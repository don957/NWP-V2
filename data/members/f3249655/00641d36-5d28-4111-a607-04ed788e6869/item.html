Name: Marsha Tyson
Badge: Layering Reading, Writing and Thinking
How does the Layering Reading, Writing, and Discussion approach compare to how you have previously supported students in engaging with multiple texts or teaching argument writing? How might this process shift or refine the way you guide students toward forming their own claims?:

I find that I have naturally used a layered writing structure with quick writes and warm-ups. This is the way a scientist thinks. Analyze evidence and make claims. When new evidence surfaces, you go back and revise or adapt a claim or explanation. Science is fluid, just like all argumentative writing approaches. It is not a formula. Throughout this course, I have thought about being intentional. Intentional in my reading practices. Intentional in the text I select. Intentional in the structures I ask students to use when making sense of text. In order to push my students and see growth in reading, writing and sense-making I know I have to be intentional about incorporating it into lessons. It is not just during a CER or argumentative writing unit.

How would you describe the role of talk and discussion in your classroom right now? What kinds of opportunities do students have to rehearse ideas through conversation? What challenges have you encountered in making talk a meaningful part of the writing process?:
Learning is loud. I find the more I talk about an issue, my beliefs may shift or refine. We have to give students the opportunity to engage in talk in the classroom. Not only does it allow them to practice summarizing thoughts, but the act of sharing can provide new ideas to their talking partners. It is also a scaffold and a model for students who may struggle with a task.

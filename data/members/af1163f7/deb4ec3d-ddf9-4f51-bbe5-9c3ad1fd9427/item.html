Name: Angie Muse
Badge: Layering Reading, Writing and Thinking
How does the Layering Reading, Writing, and Discussion approach compare to how you have previously supported students in engaging with multiple texts or teaching argument writing? How might this process shift or refine the way you guide students toward forming their own claims?:

Well, I have to go back to when I first learned about this? Was it 2016? I don't remember. But I do remember my whole thinking shifted. I was used to the typical method of here's the topic: uniforms in school. What do you believe? Find some evidence to support your thinking.

What disservice to students.

Also, I would find these pro-con articles that would give one side of an issue with bullet points, followed by the other side. It was basically Cliff's notes. Just take the points from the side you are on use them in your essay.

What I was doing, was teaching them now to organize thoughts into an essay. I wasn't actually teaching them how to THINK!! I certainly wasn't teaching them how to develop an argument from sources.

Of course, deep diving into an issue (one with multiple possible perspectives and not just agree and disagree) is the primary way we come up with a stance.

How would you describe the role of talk and discussion in your classroom right now? What kinds of opportunities do students have to rehearse ideas through conversation? What challenges have you encountered in making talk a meaningful part of the writing process?:
Well, I don't have a K-12 classroom any more. Talk and discussion are extensions of thinking. And writing is thinking. The prewriting stage of the writing process is often minimized to a chart or outline at the very beginning of the process. In actuallity, prewriting is a lot of thinking and talking and it is peppered throughout the process. Now, I am teaching two courses at UMSL. One of them is asynchronous and online. I try to use VoiceThread to get discussions goig. In my other class, we are essentially a writing group and discussion is a regular part of what we do in between drafting

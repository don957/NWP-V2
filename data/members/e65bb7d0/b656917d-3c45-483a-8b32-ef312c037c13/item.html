Which strategy from this module (Daily Schedule, Take a Line for a Walk or Interpreting Visual Texts) feels most immediately usable in your teaching context? Why?:

Honestly, all of these strategies can be implemented right away, and are pertinent to the teaching in which I'm currently involved. My favorite is probably "Take a Line for a Walk." I think using this strategy will help my students engage with the texts we are using at that moment, as well as providing opportunities to make deep connections with previous texts and other outside pieces. It is very important to me that students be presented with opportunities (nudged, sometimes) to think in ways that feel freeing and personal. This strategy gives students chances to really share their thoughts and personalities, while keeping involved with all of the things actually happening in class.

What other strategies or approaches have you used to engage your students in routine writing over the course of this school year? What, if any, challenges have you encountered in engaging students in sustained routine writing?:
I have implemented multiple strategies explored in these modules, and have enjoyed all of them (no kidding!). I think Quicklists is my favorite. Using this strategy really gives me the opportunity to tailor my approach to a variety of writing assignments and purposes. My students have also really enjoyed doing these Quicklists, and have had a lot of fun sharing their results with each other. The only challenge I have encountered is helping students overcome their "timer paralysis." Sometimes, the knowledge that time contraints are in place causes a few members of the class to stall; they have to learn to relax and not try to force themselves. This is actually a valuable life lesson, in my opinion. They need to give their bests, and that is okay. Each year, I try to teach them exactly that.

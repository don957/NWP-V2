What is one insight you gained from completing the Atwoodian Table yourself that might shape how you introduce it to students?:

Basing my own perspective on the information gained from multiple sources, not just one or two, is really important. I want to help my students process what they read in meaningful ways, and I think this activity lends itself perfectly to that. In fact, with MAP testing coming soon, I really want to work with my teammates to incorporate this strategy in preparing them (especially eighth graders) to gather information/insights and base a written response on those. Because they aren't just sort of passively finding facts, they will engage at a much deeper level with their texts. I also look forward to using this strategy as a basis for helping my students understand and appreciate perspectives as they read a novel that is written from several perspectives. I feel it'll be an easy approach to incorporate, and the students will actually enjoy doing it.

How does the Atwoodian Table idea compare to how you have taught argument and/or responding to multiple texts in the past?:
In the past, it has been a bit difficult to have students interact with one another as they respond to multiple texts. I'm interested in posting giant sticky notes around my room with the tables and chairs, then having students place their own responses around it (smaller sticky notes). Having them up and moving, then responding to others will add depth and interest.
What do you notice about the two sets of excerpts in this module (children in fancy restaurants and toddlers and technology)? How do the excerpts work together to invite students to join the conversation? What are some conversations related to topics you teach?:
These are both issues students are likely to have strong opinions about. They are "real world" topics, and I believe students will find them inviting. Because they aren't overly "adult" topics, they will be especially appealing. Many of my students have younger siblings or other relatives, and I'm interested to see how they will react to topics that hit close to home. My teammate and I just taught Out of the Dust to seventh grade, and I feel it topics such as the effects of changing family dynamics, facing adversity, find ways to cope with difficulties, etc. could easily be addressed using this strategy. As we enter a unit related to dictatorship/government control, propaganda, family dynamics, world changes since Covid, etc., I'm excited to see what topics will be best for using with the Atwoodian Table approach.

Badge: Joining a Conversation
What is one insight you gained from completing the Atwoodian Table yourself that might shape how you introduce it to students?:

I LOVED this strategy, it is probably my favorite one so far. I liked how I was able to look at two opinions. When I was younger, I struggled with engaging in opinion or argumentative texts because I felt embarrassed or intimidated if I didn't agree with the most "popular" side. This strategy gives multiple perspectives so it took some of the pressure off. I would introduce it to students as a way to have a 'low-stakes' opinion. Even if you don't agree with one source, you might agree with the next!

How does the Atwoodian Table idea compare to how you have taught argument and/or responding to multiple texts in the past?:
Honestly, it doesn't compare at all. I have always struggled with responding to multiple texts. I normally do boring questions... this is so much more engaging.
What do you notice about the two sets of excerpts in this module (children in fancy restaurants and toddlers and technology)? How do the excerpts work together to invite students to join the conversation? What are some conversations related to topics you teach?:
Both of these excerpts are easy conversations and neither one is an overly sensitive topic. They both connect since they are about children. This makes it a smooth transition between excerpts because they focus on one age group. I teach ELA so I have a lot of conversations... all the time! Right now we are talking a lot about Dystopian literature and our government. I also love connecting history to my units and have conversations about historical context and lens. Right now, we are having many conversations about the American Dream as we prep for Of Mice and Men.

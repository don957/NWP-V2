Which strategy from this module (Writer’s Notebook, Quicklists, or Writing Sprints) feels most immediately usable in your teaching context? Why?:

All three strategies could be used in my classroom, but Writing Sprints is the most immediately usable because it requires the least amount of prep. Writer's Notebook seems great, but it would require a lot of maintenance and planning before implementing. Quicklists would definitely be valuable, but the prompt elements would have to be adjusted to fit before, during, after reading strategies or intentionally constructed to connect with a creative writing prompt.

What do you anticipate will be the biggest challenge in bringing daily writing into your classroom, and how might you address it?:

Student apathy, disengagement, lack of reading skill, lack of writing skill, and lack of preparation are consistent challenges to daily writing in the classroom. To address these challenges, teachers can try low-stakes writing, connecting tasks to personal experience instead of directly to content, using timed writing, not requiring full sentences, allowing students to write informally, telling students to not worry about grammar, etc. For many years, I used Writing Revolution frames as bellwork to check for understanding, and it was always a struggle, but more open-ended writing is/was more achievable.

Looking back on this inquiry cycle, what did you learn about your teaching and your students that you might not have noticed otherwise? How did engaging in this teacher-research process shape or shift your thinking about your classroom practice?:

Teaching this layered writing sequence helped me realize how often I’ve asked students to jump to a claim too quickly. In the past, I would have them pick a side early and then go find evidence, but this process really showed me the value of slowing down and letting their thinking develop over time. I noticed that my students were more engaged in the topic because they didn’t feel locked into one opinion right away. They were more open during discussions and more willing to reconsider their ideas as we moved through each text. It also reminded me that they need support not just with writing, but with making connections between sources, especially during the mapping stage.

Think back to a moment during the lesson sequence that stands out. It could be a comment from a student, an unexpected challenge or success, a shift in engagement, or a moment when your own thinking changed. Describe what happened and why this moment feels meaningful to you now. What does it reveal:
One moment that really stood out to me was during a class discussion after the third text. A student said something along the lines of, “What if helping one species actually messes up something else?” That comment shifted the conversation, and other students started jumping in with their own thoughts and questions. It stood out because it showed that they were no longer just repeating information from the articles. They were actually thinking critically and questioning the issue. It also made me realize that the layering process was working because they had enough background from all three texts to start pushing beyond surface level ideas.
As you look over the drafts your students produced in this lesson sequence, what early patterns, surprises, or questions are emerging for you? What aspects of your students’ writing are you most curious to explore more deeply with colleagues during our April regroup? What, if any, questions do you h:
Looking at their drafts, I noticed a range in how students handled the task. Some students clearly showed growth in their thinking, referencing multiple texts and even acknowledging different perspectives before stating their claim. Others were still more basic, mostly summarizing one or two texts instead of really connecting them. I also saw that many students were more comfortable explaining their thinking than directly citing evidence, which is something I want to continue working on. Overall, their writing felt more thoughtful and less rushed than what I typically see when we go straight into an argument.

What is one insight you gained from completing the Atwoodian Table yourself that might shape how you introduce it to students?:

One insight I gained from completing the Atwoodian Table myself is how much it actually slowed down my thinking in a productive way, forcing me to consider perspectives I wouldn’t normally think about. Because of that, I would introduce it to students as a tool for exploring ideas rather than just completing an assignment, emphasizing that it’s okay to sit with multiple viewpoints before forming an opinion. I’d also model the process so they can see how thinking can evolve as different sides are considered.

How does the Atwoodian Table idea compare to how you have taught argument and/or responding to multiple texts in the past?:
Compared to how I’ve taught argument or multiple texts in the past, the Atwoodian Table feels much more intentional and structured in helping students consider different perspectives. Previously, I often had students identify claims and evidence or compare texts, but they still tended to stick to one side without fully exploring alternatives. This approach encourages deeper thinking by making multiple viewpoints a central part of the process, which I think would lead to more balanced and thoughtful responses.
What do you notice about the two sets of excerpts in this module (children in fancy restaurants and toddlers and technology)? How do the excerpts work together to invite students to join the conversation? What are some conversations related to topics you teach?:
I notice that both sets of excerpts—children in fancy restaurants and toddlers and technology; present contrasting perspectives on familiar, real-life situations, which naturally invites discussion and makes the topics more engaging for students. Instead of offering one “right” answer, they encourage students to consider multiple viewpoints and see themselves as part of the conversation. This approach could easily connect to topics I teach, like climate change, social media use, or school policies, where students can explore different perspectives and support their ideas with evidence.

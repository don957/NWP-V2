Name: Baily Stevenson
Badge: Layering Reading, Writing and Thinking
How does the Layering Reading, Writing, and Discussion approach compare to how you have previously supported students in engaging with multiple texts or teaching argument writing? How might this process shift or refine the way you guide students toward forming their own claims?:

The Reading, Writing, and Discussion approach adds reflection to what was already multiple texts with similar ideas. It encourages them to think about their thinking. This process provides the alternate resources from different perspectives and gives students time to consider what each has to offer with minimal risk.

How would you describe the role of talk and discussion in your classroom right now? What kinds of opportunities do students have to rehearse ideas through conversation? What challenges have you encountered in making talk a meaningful part of the writing process?:
Right now, I use 4-Square Discussion and have a set of rules designed for class discussion that promote active listening and minimal arguments. They don't get many opportunities to think about what they are going to say before they say it. It would be a good idea to present the idea ahead of time, allow them to write, then share to the class. When we talk through things as a class, I do run into the problem of students getting sidetracked, talking while others are talking, and students making inappropriate comments about the topic.

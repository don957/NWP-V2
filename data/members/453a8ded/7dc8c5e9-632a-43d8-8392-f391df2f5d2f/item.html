Badge: Reading to Write: They Say / I Say
Which version of the They Say / I Say routine (with visual texts or with annotation) feels like the best fit for your students right now? Why?:

The version of the They Say/I Say routine that would work best for my students right now is the annotation technique. I love this! We are pushing informational texts this year, so we are frequently close reading articles and other informational texts to build comprehension skills. Utilizing this activity by sharing an article on a Google Doc and instructing students to use the They Say/I Say templates would be an excellent way to introduce informational material, to increase comprehension skills, and to improve the important analysis skills required to complete the They Say/I Say framework. Adding the collaborative nature of this activity will help students with discussion skills and will open their minds to other mindsets. I love that this strategy challenges readers to consider what a text is saying implicitly, as this is something my students struggle with.

Describe one way you might adapt the They Say / I Say framework to align with the types of texts your students read most often (e.g., articles, visuals, multimodal texts).:

We are currently reading The Crucible, which was written as an allegory comparing McCarthyism to the Salem witchcraft trials. One of the primary goals I have for this unit is to make students think critically about the moral issues that led to both events so they can be cognizant of them as they become the future of our nation. I could use this collaborative annotation strategy to introduce informational materials about McCarthyism while facilitating deep thinking and conversation about the issue. I suspect they will conclude that both events occurred due to similar moral issues.

What, if anything, do you need help thinking through as you plan for using They Say / I Say in your classroom?:

I love how engaging the They Say/I Say visual routine is, but I'm having trouble finding ways to incorporate it in a rigorous manner with my 11th and 12th grade students.

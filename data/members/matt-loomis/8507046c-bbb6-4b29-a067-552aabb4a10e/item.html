Name: Matt Loomis
Badge: Reading to Write: They Say / I Say
Which version of the They Say / I Say routine (with visual texts or with annotation) feels like the best fit for your students right now? Why?:

Annotation seems like a better fit right now. The students already do a lot of reading and taking notes, so this strategy would be a more natural progression. However, I think using visual texts would be really valuable. I have never been able to establish a system for image analysis, and I think They Say / I Say is a potentially workable structure that would elevate student learning.

Describe one way you might adapt the They Say / I Say framework to align with the types of texts your students read most often (e.g., articles, visuals, multimodal texts).:

Students read the textbook most commonly in my class. To use They Say / I Say, I would copy a lesson from the text, which is typically two pages in length, and ask the students to read and annotate following the established framework.

What, if anything, do you need help thinking through as you plan for using They Say / I Say in your classroom?:

Seeing classroom examples of They Say / I Say in different contexts and content areas would be helpful.

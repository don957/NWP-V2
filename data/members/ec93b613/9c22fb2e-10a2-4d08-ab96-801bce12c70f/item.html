Looking back on this inquiry cycle, what did you learn about your teaching and your students that you might not have noticed otherwise? How did engaging in this teacher-research process shape or shift your thinking about your classroom practice?:

Looking back on this inquiry cycle, I learned that my students are much more capable of engaging in complex thinking and writing when they are given the right supports and structure, like sentence stems, chunked texts, and collaborative discussion opportunities. I also noticed that small instructional shifts such as adding visuals, breaking down readings, or providing multiple entry points can make a big difference in student confidence and participation, especially for reluctant writers.

Engaging in this teacher research process really shifted my thinking about pacing and flexibility in the classroom. Instead of feeling tied to a single plan or product, I found myself more willing to adjust in the moment based on student response, which ultimately led to stronger engagement and higher-quality student work.

Badge: Reading to Write: They Say / I Say
Which version of the They Say / I Say routine (with visual texts or with annotation) feels like the best fit for your students right now? Why?:

I am already planning to use this next week. We are starting to get into state testing focused skills for reading and comprehending information texts. I have to give them a practice assessment next Friday, so I started this week off with a layered synthesis activity on the Teen Brain. Next week, I will introduce the visual texts version of They Say/I Say first, then do the chunking group activity with another teen brain article, and finally have them individually do an article of the week (also on the teen brain) using They Say/I Say annotations and then writing their two paragraphs. Hopefully, then, when they do the EOC practice on Friday, they can continue practicing this protocol with it.

Describe one way you might adapt the They Say / I Say framework to align with the types of texts your students read most often (e.g., articles, visuals, multimodal texts).:

We are doing independent reading right now, so I think it would be great, after practicing this with the visual texts, and then the two articles, maybe we take a page of their fiction book and interact there, as well. We will definitely continue using this protocol with articles (probably weekly) to work on that informational text piece that they are struggling with.

What, if anything, do you need help thinking through as you plan for using They Say / I Say in your classroom?:

I don't think I need help at this point. I'm trying it out next week, so I can see how it goes and adjust from there!

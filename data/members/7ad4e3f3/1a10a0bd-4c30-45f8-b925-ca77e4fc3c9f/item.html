Name: Ashley Gregoire
Badge: Layering Reading, Writing and Thinking
How does the Layering Reading, Writing, and Discussion approach compare to how you have previously supported students in engaging with multiple texts or teaching argument writing? How might this process shift or refine the way you guide students toward forming their own claims?:

The Layered Reading, Writing, and Discussion supports previous lessons I have concluded. For instance, I played the Youtube video about the Longest missing case in the history: The disappearance of 117 Roanoke Colonists. Students took notes on the youtube video and examined evidence from Spanish Captain Coronas and John White himself. Students also read articles about historians viewpoints and students started formulating their own viewpoints to what happened to the colonists. Some said they joined local Hatteras tribe or they starved to death because they didn't have any provinces. By using this process, it guides students with resources to help formulate their own claim and developing their writing abilities.

How would you describe the role of talk and discussion in your classroom right now? What kinds of opportunities do students have to rehearse ideas through conversation? What challenges have you encountered in making talk a meaningful part of the writing process?:
I would present a claim and use the "chat" table for students to discuss the viewpoints of the claim. This will give students the opportunity to listen other viewpoints then just going with what they believe. I use articles and primary sources to help students support their viewpoints. The only challenging part of their writing process is some of my students have low reading comprehension and need assistance with understanding texts.

Name: Glenita Montgomery
Badge: Reading to Write: They Say / I Say
Which version of the They Say / I Say routine (with visual texts or with annotation) feels like the best fit for your students right now? Why?:

I wanted to start with They Say / I Say using visual texts from our math textbook because students have a hard time reading the math content and making sense of it. I want to allow them more opportunities to make connections between formulas and procedures, story problems and mathematical vocabulary that pertains to the topics we are studying.

Describe one way you might adapt the They Say / I Say framework to align with the types of texts your students read most often (e.g., articles, visuals, multimodal texts).:

We have the Savvas Realize math resources which are very heavy on reading to understand concepts. They do provide images and illustrations with examples of various problems but the students don't seem able to make connections between those concepts. By reading our textbook and having students jot down what they think the book is asking them to do, we can make connections between previously learned concepts and new materials.

What, if anything, do you need help thinking through as you plan for using They Say / I Say in your classroom?:

I pretty much have to read everything in the lessons ahead of time and make notes of what the students may be confused about especially with vocabulary concepts. Then I make a note-taking worksheet that allows them to respond to what they are thinking and what it may connect to from previous lessons and even grades. Then we talk through what doesn't make sense and try to come up with visual examples that they can put in their math journals to refer back to later.

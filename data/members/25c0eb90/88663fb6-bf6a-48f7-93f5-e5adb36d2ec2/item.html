Name: Sarah Almon
Badge: Layering Reading, Writing and Thinking
How does the Layering Reading, Writing, and Discussion approach compare to how you have previously supported students in engaging with multiple texts or teaching argument writing? How might this process shift or refine the way you guide students toward forming their own claims?:

In the past, the process for any sort of argumentative writing has been somewhat chaotic. It has also been difficult for students to keep all of their research and graphic organizers together as we make our way through the different texts. This year, the process streamlined things for us, and we had a much clearer focus as we went through the increasingly complex texts. The added benefit of students responding to the texts helped them adjust their claims as we went.

How would you describe the role of talk and discussion in your classroom right now? What kinds of opportunities do students have to rehearse ideas through conversation? What challenges have you encountered in making talk a meaningful part of the writing process?:
Talk and discussion can be a bit difficult at times because of the combinations of students I have this year. We don't get to do this as often. Throughout a unit, I try to have at least 3 planned moments for students to talk and discuss what we are learning/reading about. One additional challenge I have faced is students not being prepared for the talk/discussion. It's hard to have a meaningful conversation when students don't have anything to back up what they are saying.

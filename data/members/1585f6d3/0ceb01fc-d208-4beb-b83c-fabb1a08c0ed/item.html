Looking back on this inquiry cycle, what did you learn about your teaching and your students that you might not have noticed otherwise? How did engaging in this teacher-research process shape or shift your thinking about your classroom practice?:

Using the Layered Writing process was an eye opening experience. I can see that the use of this process vs. “what I’ve always done” has been almost limiting students. By having them read multiple perspectives on topics and discuss/reflect with peers, they are thinking about texts with more depth and digging deeper into author’s ideas. In the future, I plan to continue using this layering technique and even expand the ideas of it into my other research projects.

Think back to a moment during the lesson sequence that stands out. It could be a comment from a student, an unexpected challenge or success, a shift in engagement, or a moment when your own thinking changed. Describe what happened and why this moment feels meaningful to you now. What does it reveal:
The stand out moment for me was when a student made a comment to me about a fact from their article. They were reading about teens and technology and the student was appalled over the fact that technology is considered to be more addictive than nicotine or drugs. This particular student led her group’s conversation around that fact and how she felt about it. This may seem like a minor detail, but it truly showed me that my students were taking in the author’s information and reflecting upon it.

What is one insight you gained from completing the Atwoodian Table yourself that might shape how you introduce it to students?:

I have done something similar to this before. However, what I liked about what we did was read text from everyone at the table and summarize their arguments. This can be done when a shorter text is provided (at least in the beginning).

How does the Atwoodian Table idea compare to how you have taught argument and/or responding to multiple texts in the past?:
The approach I have used in the past was much longer text. They fatigue and don’t make it thru, especially when they have multiple articles to read. A twist on this could be a team reads one article and produces the short summary for all of the groups to read. Then individuals can further produce their own summary around the table.
What do you notice about the two sets of excerpts in this module (children in fancy restaurants and toddlers and technology)? How do the excerpts work together to invite students to join the conversation? What are some conversations related to topics you teach?:
Curating text sets is the first obstacle. I have mentioned some of my ideas already, but finding a good balance of length, technical terms, and relevancy is the goal. It takes time to curate good text sets. I wonder if AI could be used to help with this (and provide citations too). Drones, alternative energies and self-driving cars fit in my physics classes.

Which strategy from this module (Daily Schedule, Take a Line for a Walk or Interpreting Visual Texts) feels most immediately usable in your teaching context? Why?:

I really liked the "Take a Line for a Walk" strategy because of the independent reading I ask students to do in my classroom. Their independent reading happens on Mondays, Wednesdays, and Fridays, and when it is over, I immediately move into the tasks of the day. This activity would allow for a little bit more of a transition and would allow students to have a bit of a mental break before moving onto the next shift. It's also short enough that I could implement this pretty regularly - at least two or three times a week.

What other strategies or approaches have you used to engage your students in routine writing over the course of this school year? What, if any, challenges have you encountered in engaging students in sustained routine writing?:
I have been working really hard to try to incorporate more timed writing opportunities where, in a single class period, without my guidance, students have to utilize a prompt and a rubric to compose a piece with a strong claim that includes smoothly integrated relevant and sufficient evidence with analysis. I can proudly say I have managed to get my students into more writing of different genres over the past few months than I probably have in years. Some of the challenges I have faced is that students sometimes get really frustrated with having to create without my guidance, but I felt like this was an important step to take after reading Jennifer Fletcher's Writing Rhetorically. I always work to have a class roundup lesson after these writing activities as well as give students descriptive feedback to keep improving. Another really hard part that has me absolutely drowning is my perfectionist personality that has created these impossible standards for me with all of these writing activities. I feel an obligation to provide extensive descriptive feedback, and now I cannot keep up in grading. Most nights, I don't get home until 5:30 or 6. Between working with students in after school tutoring and trying to grade, It just feels like so much. I am trying to find a better balance where students are writing, feel supported in their writing journeys, get descriptive feedback from me and I somehow manage to still have a life that doesn't just revolve around teaching. I am so energized by what I am doing, but I also know I have to find a better approach that takes some of the workload off of me or I am going to burnout. If you have any advice, please send a life raft my way. :)

Looking back on this inquiry cycle, what did you learn about your teaching and your students that you might not have noticed otherwise? How did engaging in this teacher-research process shape or shift your thinking about your classroom practice?:

Looking back on using quick lists, this strategy revealed how students generate ideas, what background knowledge they bring to a topic, and where they get stuck before writing even begins. I noticed that some students had strong ideas but struggled with organization or academic language, while others needed more support just getting started. This helped me see that writing difficulties were often rooted in planning and processing rather than effort or motivation.

What new questions or next steps are you leaving this module with? How might you continue to explore or build on this inquiry in your future teaching?:

I have new questions about how to gradually release support so students rely less on scaffolds like quick lists and visual organizers while still maintaining strong writing outcomes. I am also wondering how to incorporate this into evidence based learning.

Moving forward, I plan to continue this inquiry by intentionally collecting student work samples, comparing writing produced with and without planning supports, and reflecting on student engagement and independence over time.

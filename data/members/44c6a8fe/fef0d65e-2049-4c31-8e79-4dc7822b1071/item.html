Name: Nicole Tipton
Badge: Reading to Write: They Say / I Say
Which version of the They Say / I Say routine (with visual texts or with annotation) feels like the best fit for your students right now? Why?:

The best fit version of They Say/I Say routine for my students would be used with annotations. This would be the best fit because we already annotate texts so often. My students are familiar with annotating and this strategy would help them enhance the annotations they are currently making with texts.

Describe one way you might adapt the They Say / I Say framework to align with the types of texts your students read most often (e.g., articles, visuals, multimodal texts).:

For my classroom, one way I would adapt the They Say/I Say framework to work with the text we use most often would be to provide sentence stems centered around our standard for that lesson. We read multiple texts when teaching different standards. We may read historical or scientific texts when teaching the main idea. Sentence stems are a great way to fit into the structure of the text and helps move students into interpretation of the text instead of just a summary.

What, if anything, do you need help thinking through as you plan for using They Say / I Say in your classroom?:

One thing I still need help thinking through is how to scaffold the transition from sentence frames to more independent writing. Students respond well to the They Say / I Say structure at first, but I want to make sure they don’t become overly dependent on the frames. I’m trying to figure out the right balance between giving them enough structure to be successful and gradually releasing responsibility so they can make claims, select evidence, and explain their thinking on their own.

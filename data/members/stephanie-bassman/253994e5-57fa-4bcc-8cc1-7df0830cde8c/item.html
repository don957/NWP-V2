Name: Stephanie Bassman
Badge: Joining a Conversation
What is one insight you gained from completing the Atwoodian Table yourself that might shape how you introduce it to students?:

I want to make sure they read and understand each of the other perspectives and who that person is adding to the table. Then have them do their bubbles. Then add themselves to the conversation with their own bubble. I think we will add many of the students' POV to a few giant tables around the room on giant post it notes. And then have them all visit the other tables for a quick bite.

How does the Atwoodian Table idea compare to how you have taught argument and/or responding to multiple texts in the past?:
They are not having to initially do any REAL research, but are still getting multiple POV's, opinions, etc. More relaxed way to start the process and add their own voice. Then they can CITE the evidence with research. But this way they HAVE to see both sides even at the start.
What do you notice about the two sets of excerpts in this module (children in fancy restaurants and toddlers and technology)? How do the excerpts work together to invite students to join the conversation? What are some conversations related to topics you teach?:
These topics are very...topical and easy for people to form opinions without listening to others or doing research. These topics are something they may already be thinking about or hearing parents debate. As we are reading Refugee - discussions can abound over immigration.

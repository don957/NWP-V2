Name: Sydney Brown
Badge: Reading to Write: They Say / I Say
Which version of the They Say / I Say routine (with visual texts or with annotation) feels like the best fit for your students right now? Why?:

Personally, I think that the They Say / I Say routine with visual texts feels like the best fit for my students right now. I have 5th graders, so I believe that the visual texts will help them be more engaged and participate more. I feel like the annotation might be too much for some of my students, overwhelming them.

Describe one way you might adapt the They Say / I Say framework to align with the types of texts your students read most often (e.g., articles, visuals, multimodal texts).:

One adaptation I will make for my learners is partner talk with the visual texts. Especially at the beginning of using this process, I am going to allow partners to talk through the first bits of They Say / I Say. I think that partner talk allows students to brainstorm together. When working together, they are more likely to be comfortable at trying a new process. We will still use partner talk to share our responses and to continue to brainstorm together.

What, if anything, do you need help thinking through as you plan for using They Say / I Say in your classroom?:

I think the main thing would be timing. When and where can I include this, and for how long each time?

Badge: Teacher as Researcher: Cultivating Literacy Routines
Looking back on this inquiry cycle, what did you learn about your teaching and your students that you might not have noticed otherwise? How did engaging in this teacher-research process shape or shift your thinking about your classroom practice?:

Looking back, I learned that my students understand the content more than I realized, but they need clearer support to explain why ideas matter. Before this inquiry, I didn’t notice how often they stayed in the “meaning” stage because it felt easier and more comfortable. This helped me see that the challenge wasn’t their ability,it was that they needed more structure and modeling from me. This process also helped me reflect on my own teaching. I realized that small changes, like giving sentence starters, showing examples, and practicing the steps more often, made a big difference in how confident my students felt. It reminded me that I need to slow down and focus on teaching the thinking process, not just the final answer

What new questions or next steps are you leaving this module with? How might you continue to explore or build on this inquiry in your future teaching?:

I am leaving this module wondering how I can better help students explain why something matters instead of stopping at what it means. I want to learn which examples, prompts, or routines will help them make deeper connections to the bigger ideas.

My next step is to keep using Say–Mean–Matter and give students more practice with the “Matter” part. I plan to try more modeling, sentence starters, and class discussions to help them think about significance. I also want to use this strategy in other areas, like essay writing and primary source analysis, to see how it supports their overall thinking.

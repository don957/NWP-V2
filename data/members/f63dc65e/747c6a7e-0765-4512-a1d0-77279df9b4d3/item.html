Which strategy from this module (Daily Schedule, Take a Line for a Walk or Interpreting Visual Texts) feels most immediately usable in your teaching context? Why?:

Interpreting visual texts feels like the most effective way to empower my students because it will provide an accessible entry point into social studies content. Many of my students struggle with grade-level reading, and visual texts such as political cartoons and diagrams will allow them to engage with ideas before feeling overwhelmed by text.

I believe this strategy will be usable in guiding students to analyze visuals using structured questions. This approach will help struggling readers build confidence by allowing them to demonstrate understanding without relying solely on heavy reading.

I will continue using short constructed responses and sentence starters to support students, challenges such as rushed writing and limited elaboration will remain. Incorporating visual text interpretation will help shift the focus from decoding text to critical thinking, making writing feel more manageable and meaningful for my students.

What other strategies or approaches have you used to engage your students in routine writing over the course of this school year? What, if any, challenges have you encountered in engaging students in sustained routine writing?:
I have used the Say–Mean–Matter strategy with students while analyzing I Have a Dream by Martin Luther King Jr. to help them move beyond surface-level reading and engage more deeply with the speech. Students first identified what King says by paraphrasing key lines in their own words, which supported comprehension for struggling readers. Next, they explained what those lines mean, focusing on the message King was communicating about equality and justice. Finally, students reflected on why the ideas matter, connecting the speech to the Civil Rights Movement and to issues they see in society today. One of the main challenges in using this strategy has been students’ reading ability and their initial understanding of the content, as some students struggle to decode complex language or grasp abstract ideas. However, breaking the text into these three clear steps has helped students gain confidence and develop a stronger understanding of the speech.

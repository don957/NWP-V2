Name: Angie Kelim
Badge: Layering Reading, Writing and Thinking
How does the Layering Reading, Writing, and Discussion approach compare to how you have previously supported students in engaging with multiple texts or teaching argument writing? How might this process shift or refine the way you guide students toward forming their own claims?:

Honestly, I don't find many opportunities for students in my classes to engage with multiple texts, and we haven't done any real argument writing. Of course, putting that information out into the world makes me feel a little "less than" as an educator, but it also gives me plenty of room for growth. I think this might be a good exercise to try and use to encourage students to consider the source of information they receive before making any final decisions as to which side they agree with on a given topic; there is so much false information out there these days.

How would you describe the role of talk and discussion in your classroom right now? What kinds of opportunities do students have to rehearse ideas through conversation? What challenges have you encountered in making talk a meaningful part of the writing process?:
I often attempt discussions in the classroom but my students tend to either quickly get off topic or shut down completely and refuse to give each other any input. It's like they're afraid to be wrong and afraid to open up and talk about subjects that make them actually think; they'd rather focus on whatever happened at lunch, or Chernobyl, or cars, or anything other than the topic at hand. I have so much work to do.

Name: Kelly Morris
Badge: Layering Reading, Writing and Thinking
How does the Layering Reading, Writing, and Discussion approach compare to how you have previously supported students in engaging with multiple texts or teaching argument writing? How might this process shift or refine the way you guide students toward forming their own claims?:

The Layering Reading, Writing, and Discussion approach is similar to strategies I have used in my history classroom when students read multiple sources and then write an argument using evidence. In the past, students often moved quickly from reading to writing a claim. This layering process feels more intentional because it slows that down and allows students to build their understanding first by examining sources like historical texts, speeches, podcasts, and documentaries. As a teacher, I like that students can gather information from different perspectives and compile their ideas into a rough draft before forming a claim. This helps them see how their thinking develops and encourages them to base their claims on the evidence they have learned rather than just their initial opinion. I believe this approach will help my students create stronger, more thoughtful historical arguments.

How would you describe the role of talk and discussion in your classroom right now? What kinds of opportunities do students have to rehearse ideas through conversation? What challenges have you encountered in making talk a meaningful part of the writing process?:
In my history classroom, talk and discussion help students process historical content and perspectives. Students often discuss readings and analyze sources such as historical texts, speeches, podcasts, and documentaries with partners or small groups before writing. These conversations give students a chance to rehearse their ideas, hear different viewpoints, and strengthen their understanding before forming a claim. One challenge I encounter is that some students struggle with reading complex historical documents or feel hesitant to share their ideas. Because of this, I try to scaffold discussions by modeling how to analyze sources and giving students time to organize their thoughts before speaking. Even with these challenges, discussion remains an important step in helping students clarify their thinking before writing.

Name: Suzann Copeland
Badge: Layering Reading, Writing and Thinking
How does the Layering Reading, Writing, and Discussion approach compare to how you have previously supported students in engaging with multiple texts or teaching argument writing? How might this process shift or refine the way you guide students toward forming their own claims?:

This is so awesome! I LOVE the layers of support for students. First, starting with a one word prompt and beginning to write all ideas on, next finding and working toward some directed research on one side of the topic. I love building upon the word, by moving up down the scaffold, using the infographic, video, podcast--- this so supports the multi modal- of universal design for learning. And then working with an article to annotate--- using modeling to show the annotation process, and to go back and forth between writing, discussing, and reading--- this marries and teaches the entire process with so many layers of support!

How would you describe the role of talk and discussion in your classroom right now? What kinds of opportunities do students have to rehearse ideas through conversation? What challenges have you encountered in making talk a meaningful part of the writing process?:
talk and discussion this year has grown exponentially, students are working in partners, collaborative groups, building things together, practicing their specking, practicing dialogic conversations. Challenges were teaching the children to have productive conversations with peers, they didn't know how to begin, sustain their talk, and to stay on the task presented them. But, the stems, the taking turns, the practice with shorter practice talking times really scaffolded this and helped grow their capacity, -- there are still ways in which to grow though-- but we have made so many gains!

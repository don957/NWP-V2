Which strategy from this module (Writer’s Notebook, Quicklists, or Writing Sprints) feels most immediately usable in your teaching context? Why?:

I already have in place where students have a Science notebook, so I think it would be very easy to incorporate writing sprints here. I think you could ask students to compare two modes of content and tell what they got from each. Which one was more beneficial to them(learning them as a student how they learn best) as well as allowing them to write what questions they still may have. I think this is where you could also try to facilitate students making connections to real life and how the topic applies to the way we live.

What do you anticipate will be the biggest challenge in bringing daily writing into your classroom, and how might you address it?:

I think feedback to students is important. I think feedback is also reassurance for some students that they are participating in class like you are asking. If they know teachers are not going to read what they write, some just won't do. I think there has to be a way to incorporate the reflections from the students into the learning for them to see the power of their writing.

Getting some kids to write is hard. So there has to be by in from the student as to why it matters that they take the time to write their thoughts down and make those connections. I think students need to be reassured that everything has their own way of thinking and that is ok.

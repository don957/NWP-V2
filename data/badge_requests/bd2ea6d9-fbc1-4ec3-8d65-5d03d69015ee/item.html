Name: Kelly Morris
Badge: Reading to Write: They Say / I Say
Which version of the They Say / I Say routine (with visual texts or with annotation) feels like the best fit for your students right now? Why?:

The version of the They say/I say routine that feels like the best fit for my students would be the visual texts. I believe that showing maps, posters, and images will allow my students who struggle with writing to gain confidence in their own voice.

Describe one way you might adapt the They Say / I Say framework to align with the types of texts your students read most often (e.g., articles, visuals, multimodal texts).:

In history, students encounter texts that communicate arguments through images rather than through written language. I believe I could modify the sentence starters to help students interpret those visual messages. By asking students to tell me what the author says and then express what they say it will give students practice in forming an argument in a "safe" learning environment

What, if anything, do you need help thinking through as you plan for using They Say / I Say in your classroom?:

The one area I would like to have more help with is how to balance structure with flexibility. I would like to give students the structured sentence starters to help them but I also don't want them to limit their voice when they are writing.

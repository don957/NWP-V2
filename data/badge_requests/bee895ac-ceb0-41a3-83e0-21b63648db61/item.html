Name: Audrey Morehead
Badge: Joining a Conversation
What is one insight you gained from completing the Atwoodian Table yourself that might shape how you introduce it to students?:

When introducing this to students I want to make sure they start out slowly with short excerpts and summaries of what they read. I don't want to overwhelm them by adding too much detail all at once. As students become more familiar with the Atwoodian Table I want to increase the expectation of their response. For example, using/adding textual evidence to their summary.

How does the Atwoodian Table idea compare to how you have taught argument and/or responding to multiple texts in the past?:
In the past we have looked at multiple primary sources giving an overall view of a person or concept. However, there were many sources involved and we were not able to look at them as a whole and draw conclusions/make inferences. In the future I would like to shorten the number of sources we look at and make more meaningful connections to the information we are looking at.
What do you notice about the two sets of excerpts in this module (children in fancy restaurants and toddlers and technology)? How do the excerpts work together to invite students to join the conversation? What are some conversations related to topics you teach?:
The two excerpts in this module give information regarding each topic, however, they do not overwhelm the reader to the point of tipping the reader to feel one way or another. These work to invite the student to the conversation by allowing the students own voice and reasoning to come out. Some conversations related to topics I teach could involve discussions over the souths secession or the Indian Removal Act.

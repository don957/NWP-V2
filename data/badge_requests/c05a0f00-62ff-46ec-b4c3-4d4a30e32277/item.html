Name: Kayla Peterman
Badge: Reading to Write: They Say / I Say
Which version of the They Say / I Say routine (with visual texts or with annotation) feels like the best fit for your students right now? Why?:

I feel the best version for my students would be to use annotations. One of the areas in ELA that I have been working to get the kids to understand is diving deeper in analyzing the text and find the purpose or theme throughout. Having the students annotate their readings with the The Say/I Say model will get my students focused more on this process of finding purpose and theme.

Describe one way you might adapt the They Say / I Say framework to align with the types of texts your students read most often (e.g., articles, visuals, multimodal texts).:

As we work through our novel units, I use extension articles/passages that pertain to the topic we are reading about. I would like to have the students analyze an article once a week using the They Say/ I Say model. I will give the students a template to fill out with this activity. After they finish filling it out, I will have them get with the students at their table and share their thoughts. This will allow me to circle around the room and listen to the different ways students analyzed their individual text pieces.

What, if anything, do you need help thinking through as you plan for using They Say / I Say in your classroom?:

I think it would best for me to keep in mind what it is I am wanting the students to take away from this activity and what concepts I am wanting them to understand. I need to assign text passages that will support what I am intending to teach.

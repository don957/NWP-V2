Badge: Reading to Write: They Say / I Say
Which version of the They Say / I Say routine (with visual texts or with annotation) feels like the best fit for your students right now? Why?:

I want to implement the They Say/I Say routine for introducing a debate. We are starting argumentative writing soon, and I anticipate that this could really help them with introducing information from their research and truly showing the opposing sides of a topic.

Describe one way you might adapt the They Say / I Say framework to align with the types of texts your students read most often (e.g., articles, visuals, multimodal texts).:

We do a lot of non-fiction in my class, and the framework seems to align more fluidly with fiction in the aspect that the author's thoughts are clearer. In non-fiction, it is often harder to assess the thoughts as they are woven into the plot. I would love to adapt the approach to fit analyzing short stories and how the author's viewpoint is embedded in the story.

What, if anything, do you need help thinking through as you plan for using They Say / I Say in your classroom?:

I think that I am in a good place now with the resources, especially the list of templates, to use as a starting point in planning activities.

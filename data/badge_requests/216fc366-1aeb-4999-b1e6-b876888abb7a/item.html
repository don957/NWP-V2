Name: Angie Fulton
Badge: Layering Reading, Writing and Thinking
How does the Layering Reading, Writing, and Discussion approach compare to how you have previously supported students in engaging with multiple texts or teaching argument writing? How might this process shift or refine the way you guide students toward forming their own claims?:

I approach argument writing with CER and usually as a result of experimental data. My goal is to see if they can tie the concepts learned with the evidence they gathered. I want the students to see the patterns, and be able to explain why they got the data they did. I often find that they don't have meaningful discussions about the data. They shut down and just get into solo mode when reflecting instead of discussing the patterns with their partners. I remind them constantly that they need to discuss their findings with each other as a way to help make sense of the data. I will have to shift my focus and be more intentional in facilitating their discussions. To do this, I am going to have to give them the time in class to have the discussions.

How would you describe the role of talk and discussion in your classroom right now? What kinds of opportunities do students have to rehearse ideas through conversation? What challenges have you encountered in making talk a meaningful part of the writing process?:
​Honesty we do a lot of think pair share and turn and talk in all of my classes. While this is beneficial, I need to branch out. I also have them do reciprocal teaching. I do enjoy the discussions I hear when we do this activity. Some of my classes engage in meaningful talk beautifully, however, some classes really struggle. I sometimes get disappointed in the lack of focused conversations at times with my larger classes. I can not be everywhere at once and with a couple classes of 32 students of varying levels, the students are not getting out of it what they should. I know I do other activities that involve talking, but my mind is drawing a blank.

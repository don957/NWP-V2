Badge: Reading to Write: They Say / I Say
Which version of the They Say / I Say routine (with visual texts or with annotation) feels like the best fit for your students right now? Why?:

Right now, I think using the They say / I Say routine with the annotation would be the best fit for my 8th grade ELA students. At this age, they're still learning how to organize their thoughts and respond to texts, so having a clear structure to guide their thinking is really helpful. Annotations let them slow down, highlight key points, and write their own responses right next to the text, which makes is easier for them to engage in a 'conversation" with what they're reading. Visual texts might be fun, but I think starting with written texts and annotations will help them build strong reading and writing habits firsts.

Describe one way you might adapt the They Say / I Say framework to align with the types of texts your students read most often (e.g., articles, visuals, multimodal texts).:

One way I might adapt the They Say / I Say framework for my 8th grade ELA students is by using it with articles and short nonfiction texts that they read in class which is already apart of our everyday curriculum. I could provide a simple annotation template where they write "They Say" to summarize the author's main points and "I Say" to respond with their own thoughts, questions, or connections. For videos, I could have students pause at key points to jot down the "They Say" ideas they notice and then write their "I Say" reactions. This way, the framework stays consistent but works for the types of texts they encounter most often.

What, if anything, do you need help thinking through as you plan for using They Say / I Say in your classroom?:

One thing I can think of that may need help thinking through is how to scaffold the process for students who struggle with summarizing the author's ideas or putting their own thoughts into words.

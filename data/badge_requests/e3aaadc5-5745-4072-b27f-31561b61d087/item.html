Name: Amanda Rowe
Badge: Layering Reading, Writing and Thinking
How does the Layering Reading, Writing, and Discussion approach compare to how you have previously supported students in engaging with multiple texts or teaching argument writing? How might this process shift or refine the way you guide students toward forming their own claims?:

I think by the layering pieces of text and the revisiting thoughts are strong components of this strategy, however is not something may students have seen. Students are used to picking a side of the argument and defending it. While I do think many teachers utilize the share with a partner, I do not think many teachers revisit what students have already recorded with the intent to add on it to, put more detail, or add counter propositions to their original thinking. I think students would agree that once they have completed the work, it was done, they are not used to adding to what they already created to make it better. I think this strategy is a great component to that class work is a work in progress and you can always add to your work to make it better. Students today do not have the stamina to continue to make a piece better, they just want it to be done. They also don't look at the opposing side of their view because that was no the side they choose to represent. I think this opens their minds to more perspectives and be ready to defend themselves from all angles.

How would you describe the role of talk and discussion in your classroom right now? What kinds of opportunities do students have to rehearse ideas through conversation? What challenges have you encountered in making talk a meaningful part of the writing process?:
I think talk and dicussion, can allow some students to stay hidden and not contribute to the conservation as much as others. I think by adding in the layers and multiple texts, students would be more confident to join the conversation and know their position on the topic.

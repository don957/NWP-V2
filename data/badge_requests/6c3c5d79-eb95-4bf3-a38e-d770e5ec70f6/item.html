Looking back on this inquiry cycle, what did you learn about your teaching and your students that you might not have noticed otherwise? How did engaging in this teacher-research process shape or shift your thinking about your classroom practice?:

Looking back at this cycle I learned to be less afraid of writing instruction. IT IS JUST JOINING THE CONVERSATION! I want my students to know their voices matter, we want their input. So often they can feel unheard and unnoticed. Writing gives them a voice in the convo, it allows them to be heard and seen and I want them to know that. They are also at such a formative time in their lives, learning how to develop their own opinions. Strategies like the atwoodian table and the layered reading, writing discussion helps students understand how to form their own opinions. It allows us as teachers to help students look at all the sides and how to be informed on this "round table" of life.

It was the discussion portion of the layered writing that helped her. She was able to talk through her ideas and I was her scribe. That helped her scaffold and realize, oh wait, I do have thoughts on this.

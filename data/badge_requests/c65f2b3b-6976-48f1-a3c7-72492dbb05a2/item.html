What is one insight you gained from completing the Atwoodian Table yourself that might shape how you introduce it to students?:

One insight that I gained from the Atwoodian Table is how much deeper the thinking has to be beyond just identifying surface-level details. It's easy to summarize what a character says or does, but it takes more effort to really analyze what that reveals about their motivations, beliefs, or growth.

Because of that, I would be more intentional when introducing it to students. I'd model one example out loud and show them the difference between a basic response and a more thoughtful one.

How does the Atwoodian Table idea compare to how you have taught argument and/or responding to multiple texts in the past?:
I've often had students read texts, annotate and then respond in writing- usually through a paragraph or essay where they compare claims and evidence. While that works, the Atwoodian Table would push students to sit with ideas longer and examine perspectives side by side in a more visible way.
What do you notice about the two sets of excerpts in this module (children in fancy restaurants and toddlers and technology)? How do the excerpts work together to invite students to join the conversation? What are some conversations related to topics you teach?:
Topics like these connect to students' lives while still requiring them to analyze multiple perspectives, use evidence, and build arguments. That's when discussions move from simple opinions to thoughtful, supported conversations.

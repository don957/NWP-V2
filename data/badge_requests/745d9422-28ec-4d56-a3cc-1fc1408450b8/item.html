Name: Hannah Mitchell
Badge: Reading to Write: They Say / I Say
Which version of the They Say / I Say routine (with visual texts or with annotation) feels like the best fit for your students right now? Why?:

I'm going to use this approach with visual texts. I'm going to use visual texts to help us understand propaganda, anti-semitism, and Islamophobia. I think images of hate are very powerful and I wanted students to think critically about what is being said in those types of "texts" so that students can recognize hate and bias when they see it.

Describe one way you might adapt the They Say / I Say framework to align with the types of texts your students read most often (e.g., articles, visuals, multimodal texts).:

I plan on using the TSIS template to help students reflect. My seventh graders are not naturally reflective in their reading so I will create "stop points" to reflect on TSIS and use sentence starters to support their thinking.

What, if anything, do you need help thinking through as you plan for using They Say / I Say in your classroom?:

Making the connect from image to text. We will be learning about the Holocaust and want reflection and conversation to be productive.

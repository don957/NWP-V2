Name: Patrick Stief
Badge: Layering Reading, Writing and Thinking
How does the Layering Reading, Writing, and Discussion approach compare to how you have previously supported students in engaging with multiple texts or teaching argument writing? How might this process shift or refine the way you guide students toward forming their own claims?:

The layering approach feels much more process-focused than how I’ve taught argument writing in the past. Usually, I’ve had students read multiple texts and then move pretty quickly to writing a claim, which can lead to them picking a side too early.

Layering slows that down and has students build and revise their thinking over time. Instead of starting with a claim, they develop one after interacting with multiple perspectives.

This would shift my teaching by helping me focus more on the thinking between reading and writing, which should lead to stronger, more informed claims.

How would you describe the role of talk and discussion in your classroom right now? What kinds of opportunities do students have to rehearse ideas through conversation? What challenges have you encountered in making talk a meaningful part of the writing process?:
Right now, talk and discussion play a supporting role in my classroom through things like turn-and-talks, group work, and lab discussions. These help students rehearse ideas before writing, especially with CER responses. One challenge is that not all students fully engage, and conversations can stay surface-level. Another issue is that good discussion doesn’t always carry over into their writing. I’d like to be more intentional about structuring talk and connecting it directly to writing.

Looking back on this inquiry cycle, what did you learn about your teaching and your students that you might not have noticed otherwise? How did engaging in this teacher-research process shape or shift your thinking about your classroom practice?:

I learned so much during this inquiry cycle, but the idea that stands out is the necessity for teachers to teach students to wonder. In the age of technology, I sometimes wonder if students ever wonder, ironic I know. When answers come easy, sometimes students seem to allow others to think for them. This layered approach at least slows this process.

Think back to a moment during the lesson sequence that stands out. It could be a comment from a student, an unexpected challenge or success, a shift in engagement, or a moment when your own thinking changed. Describe what happened and why this moment feels meaningful to you now. What does it reveal:
We were working on educational research papers, and one group chose to write about whether vaccines should be required for school attendance. As we created our "seats at the table" and read articles in a layered approach, I had one student realize that she could not find any research to support her claim. It was refreshing to see her realize and admit she was wrong.

Well, I am a little late to the party to comment here. But seriously, my partner teachers and I did explore the idea of student choice vs. group choice. On one hand, we want our students to choose their topics, but on the other, we recognize the power in collaboration. We decided a balanced approach where students with like interests are grouped in 3 - 4 and they choose their topics together works best.

Looking back on this inquiry cycle, what did you learn about your teaching and your students that you might not have noticed otherwise? How did engaging in this teacher-research process shape or shift your thinking about your classroom practice?:

Looking back on this inquiry cycle, I learned that the Layered Writing Model helped many of my students better organize their thinking and build their ideas step by step. By breaking writing into clear layers, I noticed that students who typically struggled with writing felt less overwhelmed and were more willing to participate in the process. I also observed that students produced more thoughtful responses based on the research they were given to review instead of them searching for it on their own and trying to come up with ideas.

Think back to a moment during the lesson sequence that stands out. It could be a comment from a student, an unexpected challenge or success, a shift in engagement, or a moment when your own thinking changed. Describe what happened and why this moment feels meaningful to you now. What does it reveal:
I remember one moment that stood out during the layered writing lesson sequence occurred when students moved from the brainstorming layer to the drafting layer. As students began writing their ideas, one student commented that the writing felt easier because they already had their ideas organized. This moment was meaningful because it showed that the layered structure was helping students approach writing with more confidence. Instead of feeling overwhelmed by the task of writing a full response, students were able to focus on one step at a time.

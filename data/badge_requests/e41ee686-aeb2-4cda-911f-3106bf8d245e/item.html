Looking back on this inquiry cycle, what did you learn about your teaching and your students that you might not have noticed otherwise? How did engaging in this teacher-research process shape or shift your thinking about your classroom practice?:

What did I learn about my teaching and my students that I hadn't noticed before this inquiry cycle?

I realized that high engagement with whatever students are reading is a worthwhile process. I know that I've had students throughout my teaching years who have struggled with reading comprehension, and I wasn't trained to deal with that, and didn't have any tools in my teaching tool-kit to help them improve that skill. I was just teaching science for the sake of science. I didn't think I was teaching in my silo, but in reality, I have been. I thought it was the ELA teachers' job to teach reading so that they could do science. Now, I see that we're all in it together. We all have a responsibility to help build students' literacy. The more close, deep reading with writing that I can do in science, the better my students will be able to comprehend any reading they do, whether it's in science, social studies, or for their own enjoyment.

How did engaging in this teacher-research process shape or shift my thinking about my classroom practice?

In the past, I have felt like I just didn't have the time for all of this reading and writing. Now, I feel like I need to make the time. It's worth it. I've known that my students didn't have the reading stamina that it takes to read the long articles in the Science World magazines. With the They Say I Say strategy, I feel like I can scaffold that process, going from whole-class reading to partner reading, and then to reading all by themselves. It will take the rest of the year, but in the end, I want them to be trained to have the grit that they need to read non-fiction, even if it's several pages long.

What new questions or next steps are you leaving this module with? How might you continue to explore or build on this inquiry in your future teaching?:

What new questions or next steps am I leaving this module with?

I don't think I have any new questions. I know that I'm just at the beginning stages of understanding and incorporating these strategies in my classroom, but I feel confident that I can get better with them with practice on my part, and for my students as well. I'm anxious to see their progression through the rest of this year. I plan to continue using it, as I said above, to guide students toward greater reading stamina, but with deeper understanding of what they've read.

How might you continue to explore or build on this inquiry in your future teaching?

I think finding the right non-fiction text from year to year will be something to explore. I can use some from year to year, but I also like to use current science to stay relevant, so that will be a challenge. I will be building more of a non-fiction text repertoire or portfolio to choose from in the future.

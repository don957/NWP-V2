How might you introduce Say-Mean-Matter to your students? What about this framework do you think will be helpful for them? What might be challenging?:

I want to introduce Say-Mean-Matter to my juniors. We are reading Into the Wild and the author, Jon Krakauer, starts each chapter with a quote. I've thought about their final being they pick 3 of the quotes from those intros and do a say-mean-matter with them. This will be a great option for my students who don't like doing the traditional essay over a prompt. I also think this is a great framework to help teach students how to make inferences from a text. I have some struggling middle schoolers who tend to regurgitate the text, giving me textual evidence, but not being able to explain what it means. This strategy will be great practice for them, forcing them to slow down and truly digest the text. I plan on giving students the quotes to start. Then I ask my more advanced students to pull their own. I think I even have small enough class sizes that I could have each student pick out one favorite quote of theirs and we all do each other's quotes.

TO ADD, I did try the say-mean-matter with my junior class. I introduced it to them with two quotes that open chapter 8 of Into the Wild. I used the Simplify Writing student examples to talk them through. I used the advanced 12th-grade class to show them how deep they can go, and then showed them the example from the Crucible. (We read that text last unit) I wanted the students to get an example of a thorough response and a shorter response so they knew it was a low stakes activity. I then modeled the say mean matter with the first quote, which is the attached picture.

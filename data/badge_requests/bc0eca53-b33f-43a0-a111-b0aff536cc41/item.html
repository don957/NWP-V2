What is one insight you gained from completing the Atwoodian Table yourself that might shape how you introduce it to students?:

One insight I gained from completing the Atwoodian Table myself is to use the speech bubbles as quotes! It makes it easy to look back and picture what the argument would have sounded like in real life. Other than that, it was presented perfectly. I plan to use it exactly as described.

How does the Atwoodian Table idea compare to how you have taught argument and/or responding to multiple texts in the past?:
In the past I have used an annotated bibliography to gather sources for argumentative essays, but I am certainly going to start using this concept to teach it! We've also used a lined venn diagram, but this concept accomplishes so much more, like the fact that each person has a different perspective rather than the idea that there are only two sides to something. It also helps them visualize themselves entering an argument that already exists rather than them thinking they have all the information and are creating the argument.
What do you notice about the two sets of excerpts in this module (children in fancy restaurants and toddlers and technology)? How do the excerpts work together to invite students to join the conversation? What are some conversations related to topics you teach?:
The excerpts appeal to different people, but they have a similar basis: children. They work together because the parents with children in fancy restaurants are probably utilizing screen time to keep their kids occupied! Conversations related to the topics I teach would be vaping, video games, technical school vs. college, gun rights, and hunting ethics.

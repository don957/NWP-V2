Name: Shaylee Rademacher
Badge: Layering Reading, Writing and Thinking
How does the Layering Reading, Writing, and Discussion approach compare to how you have previously supported students in engaging with multiple texts or teaching argument writing? How might this process shift or refine the way you guide students toward forming their own claims?:

I feel like I keep saying the same thing over and over. I haven't done anything like this before. I teach using multiple texts, short videos, etc, but kind of like stand-alone learning opportunities. As I said in the last module, I haven't taught argument writing in the past, mostly because I've felt like my content hasn't led to argument. I've also held the belief that 7th graders haven't developed the reasoning part of the brain, so writing their reasoning at the end of a C-E-R has been limited to a guided response. Having admitted all of this, when I have given students the chance to write their own reasoning (scientific justification) I have been pleasantly surprised by the results from them. Granted, they were writing these as a group, with one or more students doing most of the work, but at least they have the exposure to the process. I need to be mindful of getting started with C-E-R earlier in the year, so that they have more practice with it. I'm definitely going to shift toward layered texts for presenting the evidence so that they have the information to develop their own claim.

How would you describe the role of talk and discussion in your classroom right now? What kinds of opportunities do students have to rehearse ideas through conversation? What challenges have you encountered in making talk a meaningful part of the writing process?:
I don't use class discussions often. We used to do Kagan strategies for guiding group discussions, but got away from it. It seemed like I was the one doing all of the work for what seemed like shallow discussions, much time wasted, and a chaotic classroom. I do still use the basic 'turn and talk,' but not often. I have been using Take a Line for a Walk every week for awhile and my students do enjoy it. I've been impressed with their focus during their timed writings. This week I am going to have students pair up with another student who read the same text, and have them discuss it, and then continue their writing after the discussion. It will be fun to see how that goes.

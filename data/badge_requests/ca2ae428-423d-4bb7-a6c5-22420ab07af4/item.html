Which strategy from this module (Daily Schedule, Take a Line for a Walk or Interpreting Visual Texts) feels most immediately usable in your teaching context? Why?:

The strategy from this module that feels most immediately usable in my teaching context is Interpreting Visual Texts. Agriculture naturally lends itself to visuals such as photos, diagrams, maps, and infographics, which help students better understand real-world concepts. Many of the visuals used in agriculture are not typical graphs but images that require students to closely observe, analyze, and make meaning from what they see. Using this strategy allows students to practice interpretation skills while engaging with content that feels authentic and relevant to agriculture.

What other strategies or approaches have you used to engage your students in routine writing over the course of this school year? What, if any, challenges have you encountered in engaging students in sustained routine writing?:
Over the course of my eight years of teaching, I have used a variety of literacy strategies to engage students in routine writing, often embedding short writing tasks such as reflections, explanations, or responses to prompts within daily lessons. However, after starting this program, I have become much more intentional about when and how I use these strategies. Rather than treating writing as an add-on, I now plan purposeful opportunities for students to write using specific frameworks and supports I have learned over the past few months. One ongoing challenge with sustained routine writing is maintaining student engagement and stamina, especially when students struggle with confidence or see writing as separate from the content. Being more deliberate with structure and strategy has helped address some of these challenges and made writing feel more meaningful for students.

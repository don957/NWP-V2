Which strategy from this module (Daily Schedule, Take a Line for a Walk or Interpreting Visual Texts) feels most immediately usable in your teaching context? Why?:

I am going to use the Interpreting Visual Texts, as I wrote about in the first part of this module, as part of the layered reading strategy, so I'll leave that for later. I would like to try the Take a Line for a Walk as a Science Starter with the Science World magazine. I think the students would enjoy having the chance to glance through the entire magazine and choose something that piques their interest. Then they can take that line for a walk and see where it takes them in their thoughts. It'll be interesting to see what happens with it.

What other strategies or approaches have you used to engage your students in routine writing over the course of this school year? What, if any, challenges have you encountered in engaging students in sustained routine writing?:
My principal was able to buy the small writer's journals similar to the ones we use at the regroups for every student in the four core classes. We finally got them distributed last Tuesday, and I used them two time last week. The students had a few minutes to decorate the front of their journals with their names, something the large marjority likes to do. We work pretty hard in Science class, bell to bell, so a little artistic time is appreciated. On the first day of each week we do what I call Thorns, Roses, and Buds, so the students are very accustomed to writing a sentence or two to tell about something that isn't great in their life (Thorn), something pretty great (Rose) and something they're looking forward to (Bud). To give them more writing on paper practice we did these in their journals and did a timed writing. Most enjoyed the opportunity to write about something with which they are very familiar, themselves. On another day, for our Science Starter (bellringer) we did a variation on the QuickList, and I had them write out a Gratitude List. Then they picked one thing on the list and wrote about it more in-depth, and I also used a timer to keep them going. I think having the journal, keeping them in all in my classroom so I don't have to deal with those who forget them in their lockers, and having set days to use them for specific writing activities will help me build a better writing habit. Not having the journals until the second semester set us back, but I think that we're off to a better start now.

Badge: Teacher as Researcher: Cultivating Literacy Routines
Looking back on this inquiry cycle, what did you learn about your teaching and your students that you might not have noticed otherwise? How did engaging in this teacher-research process shape or shift your thinking about your classroom practice?:

I learned that my students are more capable than I gave them credit for. I told them we were going to try some new writing strategies, and they jumped right on board with me, even when I fumbled with some of the lessons. For myself, I learned that I needed a refresher in writing lessons. I tend to get bogged down by the 5-paragraph essay, and never revert from that. Now, after trying some of the new strategies, I'm thinking of how bored my students were with my writing lessons. I think I forgot that writing is creative and should be approached as such. My students love to write as much as I do, and playing around with some of the strategies I've learned has helped me bring fun writing and learning back into my classroom.

What new questions or next steps are you leaving this module with? How might you continue to explore or build on this inquiry in your future teaching?:

I'm thinking about how I can incorporate some of the writing strategies I've learned into my novel units. I did that with the Say - Mean- Matters strategy, which I think worked well, but I want to experiment with other strategies using our novel units. I think the I Say/You Say could work well as a Character Says/I Say writing lesson with one of our novels. I think there could be many connections and deeper level thinking with my novel units by using that particular strategy. I'm ready and excited to get a lot more creative with my lesson plans, using the strategies I've learned.

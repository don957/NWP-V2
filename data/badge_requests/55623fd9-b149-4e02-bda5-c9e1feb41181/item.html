Name: Phoebe Pettit
Badge: Layering Reading, Writing and Thinking
How does the Layering Reading, Writing, and Discussion approach compare to how you have previously supported students in engaging with multiple texts or teaching argument writing? How might this process shift or refine the way you guide students toward forming their own claims?:

In the past, supporting students with multiple texts or argument writing in an agriculture class has often been more informal and discussion-based, with less emphasis on structured argumentative writing. The Layering Reading, Writing, and Discussion approach feels more intentional and supportive because it breaks the process into clear steps and gives students repeated exposure to ideas before they are asked to form a claim. This process could refine the way I guide students by helping them build background knowledge, understand different perspectives, and use evidence more confidently, even if full argumentative writing is not always the end goal.

How would you describe the role of talk and discussion in your classroom right now? What kinds of opportunities do students have to rehearse ideas through conversation? What challenges have you encountered in making talk a meaningful part of the writing process?:
Right now, talk and discussion play a limited but important role in my classroom. Whole-class discussions can be challenging because they are easily disrupted, so I tend to rely more on structured conversations. I’ve tried using paired or small-group discussions with guiding papers or prompts so students have something concrete to focus on while they talk. These opportunities allow students to rehearse ideas before writing, but the biggest challenge is keeping discussions purposeful and on task. With more clear structures and expectations, talk could become a stronger and more meaningful part of the writing process in my classroom.

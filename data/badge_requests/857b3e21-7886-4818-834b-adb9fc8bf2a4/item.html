Badge: Reading to Write: They Say / I Say
Which version of the They Say / I Say routine (with visual texts or with annotation) feels like the best fit for your students right now? Why?:

I think I would use the They Say/I Say routine with annotation at this current moment in time with my students. We are about to begin looking at texts for an upcoming argumentative unit, and this would be a perfect way to annotate the different articles we will be using. With argumentative reading and writing, students have to be able to distinguish between opposing sides of an argument, and this is often difficult for my 6th graders to do. They Say/I Say might help them better sort through different sides of an argument.

Describe one way you might adapt the They Say / I Say framework to align with the types of texts your students read most often (e.g., articles, visuals, multimodal texts).:

My students often work with full length articles in the classroom. However, it might benefit my students to practice the They Say/I Say routine with them while looking at these articles in smaller chunks. Many of the articles we read are divided into small sub sections, so this would make it fairly simple to chunk up the texts. When doing the actual annotations, I would also have my students do some color coding. For instance, using a yellow highlighter to symbolize that they "they say" portion is something they agree with and a pink highlighter symbolizes a portion they disagree with. Then, when the students refer back to the articles later on in the unit, they can more easily find the information that they need to reference.

Looking back on this inquiry cycle, what did you learn about your teaching and your students that you might not have noticed otherwise? How did engaging in this teacher-research process shape or shift your thinking about your classroom practice?:

This process clearly reminded me that most of my students are capable of so much more than they are given at times. Even though I have students who complain at times about work being too hard, I really feel that more of them truly enjoy being challenged and thrive on learning more about things they know a little about.

Think back to a moment during the lesson sequence that stands out. It could be a comment from a student, an unexpected challenge or success, a shift in engagement, or a moment when your own thinking changed. Describe what happened and why this moment feels meaningful to you now. What does it reveal:
One thing that stood out during our discussions of the third article within Teens and Technology, was the discussion of the positives and supports that can come from different types of "screentime." Kids are so much more knowledgeable about technology than I am, and I learned new things in every one of my six classes during our discussions. They were so honest with themselves and one another when they were discussing the amount of time spent in front of a screen each day. Some of them even mentioned that they totally understood why their parents limited their phone usage times. They whole-heartedly became more involved in the articles than I expected them to be.

I am really questioning what happened. They had written so many short argumentative pieces of writing, but they had a really hard time writing this one after all of the wonderful conversations and hard work preparing. I really think it had a lot to do with the topic. For some reason, using the word technology caused the kids to question what that actually meant. We had talked a lot about the fact that they could be writing about anything they used a screen for. One pattern I’m noticing across student drafts is that many students can state a claim about teens and technology, but they definitely need more help being able to pull evidence that corresponds. I need to find better ways to teach them how to do that.

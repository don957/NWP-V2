Badge: Reading to Write: They Say / I Say
Which version of the They Say / I Say routine (with visual texts or with annotation) feels like the best fit for your students right now? Why?:

I think I would start with the images. I think it might be an easier way to jump in. We can also talk about how authors put messages in images as well and lead into a discussion about images in articles, texts, etc. I know when I have MRI training she talks a lot about reading around the text before you actually read the text.

Describe one way you might adapt the They Say / I Say framework to align with the types of texts your students read most often (e.g., articles, visuals, multimodal texts).:

We read and analyze a lot of data/graphs in science. I think we could adapt it by having students use "They say" to say what the graph or data is showing. Just what they see. The "I say" part could then be either the student explaining the concept that is happening or why they think the graph or data is showing up in the way it is. I would like to add more texts where they are looking into current science events and they could then make their comments on those and how it relates to what we are learning.

What, if anything, do you need help thinking through as you plan for using They Say / I Say in your classroom?:

I'm not sure. I think it will just take practice but I think it will be a beneficial tool in my classroom. I think that the sentence starters and frameworks will also help my students and give them a place to start.

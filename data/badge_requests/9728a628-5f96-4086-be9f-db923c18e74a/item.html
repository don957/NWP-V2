What is one insight you gained from completing the Atwoodian Table yourself that might shape how you introduce it to students?:

I think that showing students that their opinions or speech is important and valuable enough to be considered alongside evaluated sources. I used this idea when explaining how to write a good concept map to my students. I encouraged them to state evidence from their sources, while also including their own thoughts alongside the evidence.

How does the Atwoodian Table idea compare to how you have taught argument and/or responding to multiple texts in the past?:
I have used "tabletop Twitter" in the past where students evaluated sources and wrote their responses on a table (big paper). After that, students circle to the papers and respond to their peers' opinions with writing, and then in one more pass, students had to go back to their original thoughts and see if they would change anything based off of their peers' responses.
What do you notice about the two sets of excerpts in this module (children in fancy restaurants and toddlers and technology)? How do the excerpts work together to invite students to join the conversation? What are some conversations related to topics you teach?:
The excerpts work together by giving students alternating opinions that our students might find their own opinions within when reading. Some conversations related to topics I teach would be ethical issues such as stem cell research or genetic engineering.

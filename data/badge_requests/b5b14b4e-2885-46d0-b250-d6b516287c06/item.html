Name: Sarah Apple
Badge: Layering Reading, Writing and Thinking
How does the Layering Reading, Writing, and Discussion approach compare to how you have previously supported students in engaging with multiple texts or teaching argument writing? How might this process shift or refine the way you guide students toward forming their own claims?:

The approach is similar to what I have done before - I start every lesson with a quick write and regularly lead them through layered texts as we learn. However, I was inspired by the document provided (with the suggested sequence) to create a table in which they record their writing and the learning process. It worked really well as we moved from one text to the next and came back to the working document to add our writing and thoughts. Having all of their writing on one document (even though it came from 4 different parts of the lesson) helped them to realize this is all connected. So it may have been unintentional inspiration but it worked so well! Thanks

How would you describe the role of talk and discussion in your classroom right now? What kinds of opportunities do students have to rehearse ideas through conversation? What challenges have you encountered in making talk a meaningful part of the writing process?:
We start every class period with a free write followed by turn and talk discussion at tables. That is supposed to then feed into whole group discussion. I also make all lessons interactive so that we are frequently pausing the lesson to answer questions, ask questions, etc. Conversation is so important to learning and yet it is, honestly, one of the biggest challenges I face. Students are very reluctant to speak up. As for making it a meaningful part of the writing process, I do prompt them to revise and/or add to their quick write after discussions. That's probably the best example of how I try to make the talking meaningful. What did you learn from the conversation and what would you change/add to your quick write now that the conversation has taken place.

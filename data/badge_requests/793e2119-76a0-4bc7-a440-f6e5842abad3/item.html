Name: Charity Bentley
Badge: Layering Reading, Writing and Thinking
How does the Layering Reading, Writing, and Discussion approach compare to how you have previously supported students in engaging with multiple texts or teaching argument writing? How might this process shift or refine the way you guide students toward forming their own claims?:

The Layered Reading, Writing, and Discussion strategy allows my students to digest information in bite-sized pieces, so to speak. They are able to ingest a piece of information, then digest and process it, which feels much more manageable than making big demands of them, like instructing them to read three pieces and form a conclusion. This type of assignment can often feel overwhelming, but this feel easy. It reminds me of the old question of "How do you eat an elephant? One bite at a time." My students will be much more engaged, because they'll want to interact with their peers and contribute to discussions. There is no big push here, so they won't shut down. Because they're truly thinking throughout the process, they will be sort of passively crafting claims as they go. I really believe this will make it much easier for me to guide them in writing claims; they will have already identified their opinions and perspectives, so only a bit of fine-tuning will be necessary.

How would you describe the role of talk and discussion in your classroom right now? What kinds of opportunities do students have to rehearse ideas through conversation? What challenges have you encountered in making talk a meaningful part of the writing process?:
I do try to incorporate talk and discussion as much as possible, although this strategy gives a great guideline for doing it often. Instead of a significant amount of time going by, leading to "big" discussion times, little bits of frequent conversation keep things moving quite smoothly and steadily. This is very helpful. In the past, I haven't been specific enough with my structure for discussions, or have given them too much to discuss at a time. This is much better!

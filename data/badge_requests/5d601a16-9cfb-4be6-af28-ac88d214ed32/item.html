Name: Sara Newberry
Badge: Layering Reading, Writing and Thinking
How does the Layering Reading, Writing, and Discussion approach compare to how you have previously supported students in engaging with multiple texts or teaching argument writing? How might this process shift or refine the way you guide students toward forming their own claims?:

I feel embarrassed about this now, but in the past I have had students choose a claim for their essays, then go find evidence to prove it. I did require them to express their understanding of the opposition, but not near as effectively as I should have. One of the biggest lessons this has taught me is that I need to make sure students know that, like the Atwoodian Table, there are endless perspectives on a topic, and that is why using evidence to back up claims is so important. The layered writing task requires them to look into the different perspectives of their argument, respond to it, compare and contrast perspectives, THEN choose where they fit at the table. This will make their arguments so much stronger. I am certainly going to utilize this task before our next essay.

How would you describe the role of talk and discussion in your classroom right now? What kinds of opportunities do students have to rehearse ideas through conversation? What challenges have you encountered in making talk a meaningful part of the writing process?:
Talking and discussing is honestly the #1 thing we do in my class. I am not a tested class (English 3 and 4), so I really invest in preparing them for real world communicating, synthesizing, comprehension, etc. We do Socratic Seminars weekly, we do debates, we do public speaking, we do speed dating and speed debating frequently to share progress, perspectives, and opinions, etc. I honestly haven't struggled with making talk a meaningful part of our class; where I do feel like my instruction has lacked is writing. I never liked writing in high school because it was never about my perspective or my creativity, so I think I assumed my students would also dread writing and I treated it like a "necessary evil" to get to the speaking tasks I assigned. I was SO wrong. Not only have I grown to love writing, but so have my students! They actually tell me how much they enjoy it, and they get so excited to share what they write!

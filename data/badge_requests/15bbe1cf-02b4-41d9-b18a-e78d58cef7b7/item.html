Looking back on this inquiry cycle, what did you learn about your teaching and your students that you might not have noticed otherwise? How did engaging in this teacher-research process shape or shift your thinking about your classroom practice?:

This really highlighted that students learn in different ways. I used a combination of videos, text and infographics in my layers and students seemed to really respond to the variety. They were all able to learn from this. Some "texts" may have provided stronger learning opportunities for some students rather than others. I think the combo of videos and articles really helped. Also we "group read" the articles and group took notes on them. Although this took more time, I think it helped ensure everyone could access the content. I also learned that students liked talking about what they were learning. In many of the previous turn and talks I have tried students can get off topic very quickly. That happened less with this. Students were able to contribute to the conversations. They may not have always had the proper structure, but the students were talking about the material. Some even got into good debates and were citing things from text. I was great to watch. I also think the talking helped with the writing. For the most part, the students did the quick writes. No one sat and just stared at the page. I think the conversation piece helped with this. Overall, it is a long process and takes careful, deliberate planning, but I think it is a good use of time. As for classroom practice, it highlighted the importance of talking and writing. I think the pairing works well and I am going to try to include more talk/write quick writes in the future.

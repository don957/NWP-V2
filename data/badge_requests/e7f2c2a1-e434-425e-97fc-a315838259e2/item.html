Name: Jessica Kennedy
Badge: Layering Reading, Writing and Thinking
How does the Layering Reading, Writing, and Discussion approach compare to how you have previously supported students in engaging with multiple texts or teaching argument writing? How might this process shift or refine the way you guide students toward forming their own claims?:

Compared to how I have previously taught multi-text reading or argument writing, the layering process places more emphasis on developing ideas over time rather than starting with a fixed claim. What feels familiar is using multiple texts and discussion to build understanding, as well as incorporating writing throughout the process, but what feels new is delaying the final claim and intentionally revisiting and revising thinking after each layer of reading. In the past, students often chose a position first and then searched for evidence, whereas this approach encourages them to explore multiple perspectives before forming an informed claim, ultimately shifting my instruction toward prioritizing deeper thinking and a more flexible, evidence-based writing process.

How would you describe the role of talk and discussion in your classroom right now? What kinds of opportunities do students have to rehearse ideas through conversation? What challenges have you encountered in making talk a meaningful part of the writing process?:
Talk and discussion in my classroom are used to help students process ideas, share perspectives, and build understanding through activities like partner discussions and whole-class conversations. I provide regular opportunities for students to talk, but I sometimes find that participation is uneven, with some students hesitant to share. A challenge is creating structures that ensure all students feel comfortable contributing while keeping discussions focused and meaningful.

Which strategy from this module (Writer’s Notebook, Quicklists, or Writing Sprints) feels most immediately usable in your teaching context? Why?:

All three of these strategies are usable in my teaching context, since I teach writing. For this, I will focus on Writer's Notebooks, but I use quick writes, which are similar to writing sprints, and I would like to start using quicklists. Everything we do in class, aside from grammar work, is done in their writer's notebooks.

What do you anticipate will be the biggest challenge in bringing daily writing into your classroom, and how might you address it?:

A lot of students get hung up with the idea that it has to be "perfect" or "right" the first time. That includes their spelling, and so I have some students that write very slowly in class because they struggle with the idea of having to get it right. I try to address this by pointing out how many writers, when writing a first draft, do not worry about those things. I also don't count off for spelling in many of the assignments I give them, especially if it is a rough draft or a quick write.

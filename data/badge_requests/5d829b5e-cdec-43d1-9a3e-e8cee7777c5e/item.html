Badge: Reading to Write: They Say / I Say
Which version of the They Say / I Say routine (with visual texts or with annotation) feels like the best fit for your students right now? Why?:

I have two different groups of adult students.

IN-SERVICE TEACHERS VIA PROFESSIONAL LEARNING.

This group was at the beginning stage of source-based argument. We were looking at routine ways to incorporate argument. I used the images with them. (Starting at slide 59 in this presentation: https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1OAY6Uha4aoPMX6X9CQNv6AVU3qKin341exep34r38xM/edit?usp=sharing)

PRE-SERVICE TEACHERS VIA COLLEGE COURSE

For this group who are working on certification across all subject areas, annotation is the right spot for them. I use writing to learn strategies to help them process course texts. Annotating each text with a different strategy not only helps them process the reading but also gives them valuable practice with the strategies they should be using in their classes.

Describe one way you might adapt the They Say / I Say framework to align with the types of texts your students read most often (e.g., articles, visuals, multimodal texts).:

For my pre-service class, I am going to replace the Talk Back strategy--which is one from our text book--with the They Say I Say strategy. I use this strategy with on chapter in particular because there are places for student to push back on what the authors are saying. After they use this strategy via annotation, I am going ask a few questions to help them think through the notion of using this strategy to 1.) see reading and responding to text as a conversation and 2.) position this strategy as the essence of academic writing. I think it will help them value the process a lot more.

What, if anything, do you need help thinking through as you plan for using They Say / I Say in your classroom?:

I am not sure that I need help, but if you can think of question I can pose to my pre-service teacher after they practice this strategy to help them see this strategy 1.) as a conversation and 2.) as a foundation for academic writing, then I would be glad to hear your thoughts.

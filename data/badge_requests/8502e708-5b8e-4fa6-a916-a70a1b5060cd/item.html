Name: Laura Jones
Badge: Reading to Write: They Say / I Say
Which version of the They Say / I Say routine (with visual texts or with annotation) feels like the best fit for your students right now? Why?:

I would say the visual texts version of this would fit my students best right now. I teach 6th graders, so having an easy way to connect this idea to them to start would be the best way to go. They already get a lot of their information from Memes and TikToks, so having short, easy text to analyze to begin is a way to build those connections.

Describe one way you might adapt the They Say / I Say framework to align with the types of texts your students read most often (e.g., articles, visuals, multimodal texts).:

We read a lot of different texts, but I do know students I have had in the past struggle with reading visuals within articles. I would use this method with that type of text, and then move on to finding more examples of similar information within the article.

What, if anything, do you need help thinking through as you plan for using They Say / I Say in your classroom?:

How would I be able to pull in students who are reluctant to interpret, or don't know where to start on the I Say portion of it. I have a few students that fear being "wrong".

Name: Stephanie Beadle
Badge: Layering Reading, Writing and Thinking
How does the Layering Reading, Writing, and Discussion approach compare to how you have previously supported students in engaging with multiple texts or teaching argument writing? How might this process shift or refine the way you guide students toward forming their own claims?:

Prior to this year, I hadn't offered any activities like this. I have given articles that were connected, but I hadn't really allowed students to deepen and explore the reading alongside writing. It was more just separate assignments. I learned about the layered technique last semester and tried it out. I used a quick write, followed by an artifact museum (different texts that they were able to peruse, like a sedentary gallery walk) and then a couple of full length articles that they then created a claim from. Unfortunately, I didn't realize that this activity needed more time and I didn't have anywhere to go from there. When I do it again, I will certainly learn from these mistakes. I'm planning to use this technique again this semester with our research and inquiry unit next month. I think the claims students wrote, and the observations they made overall, last semester were impressive. I think this technique allows for depth and I hope it helps students understand the importance of taking the time to look at lots of evidence.

How would you describe the role of talk and discussion in your classroom right now? What kinds of opportunities do students have to rehearse ideas through conversation? What challenges have you encountered in making talk a meaningful part of the writing process?:
I struggle with discussion, overall, and I have my students at tables that we build community at throughout the year. My seniors are currently struggling to care at all, so, for example, today I had them do a whip around so they would all participate in conversation. But most of sophomore classes have no problem discussing at their tables. I have them share after quick writes and work collaboratively on activities like sentence scramblers. My biggest challenge with discussion is always classes that don't want to talk. The quiet ones or the ones with just one or two students who always contribute. As a socially anxious introvert myself, I struggle to make anyone speak in front of the group, but I know it is good for them. Still working on different protocols to help with this, but it has gotten better over the years.

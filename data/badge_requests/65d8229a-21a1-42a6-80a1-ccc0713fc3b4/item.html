Badge: Teacher as Researcher | Sustaining Literacy Routines
Looking back on this inquiry cycle, what did you learn about your teaching and your students that you might not have noticed otherwise? How did engaging in this teacher-research process shape or shift your thinking about your classroom practice?:

I have learned that even though I do reading interventions, there are easy, practical ways to integrate writing and discussion into my groups. Using low-stakes writing strategies/practices in writer’s notebooks is an easy way to connect reading, writing, and discussion. Using visual texts is a great way to scaffold for students who are in my reading intervention groups. If I had not done this inquiry cycle, I would not have known all the ways I can get students to write and engage in discussion about their writing. I would not have known practical low-stakes strategies to get students engaged in reading, writing, and discussion. I plan to integrate the layered writing lessons with Lexia Aspire to not only improve students' reading, but also their writing skills. It has made me realize that I need to incorporate more writing and discussion into my lessons to support the learners I work with.

Think back to a moment during the lesson sequence that stands out. It could be a comment from a student, an unexpected challenge or success, a shift in engagement, or a moment when your own thinking changed. Describe what happened and why this moment feels meaningful to you now. What does it reveal:
One thing that stood out to me was when students created a visual while mapping the conversation. One student in particular, who is reluctant to write, was excited to create a visual representation of the conversation. This was meaningful because they were applying and engaging with the texts. They were making connections with their own opinions. It was a low-stakes process that engaged the students with the argument at hand. The layering process gave students multiple attempts to engage in the argument about school start time and created meaningful discussion. I have several ELL students in my intervention groups, and this process was a great way for them to engage in oral discussion. One thing I would do differently next time is provide my students with text that is closer to their level.

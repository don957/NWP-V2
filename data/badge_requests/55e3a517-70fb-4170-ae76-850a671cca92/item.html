Name: Gretchen Haer
Badge: Reading to Write: They Say / I Say
Which version of the They Say / I Say routine (with visual texts or with annotation) feels like the best fit for your students right now? Why?:

Annotation feels like the best fit for my students and me right now because it works well with our classroom resource/curriculum. We have StudySync through McGraw Hill, and the margins in the student consumables are huge for annotation. It makes it very simple for my students to respond to the text right there on the text with they say, I say.

Describe one way you might adapt the They Say / I Say framework to align with the types of texts your students read most often (e.g., articles, visuals, multimodal texts).:

I have specific texts with my curriculum that I believe would be great for they say/ I say. for example, my sophomores have the text Ramayana, which is an incredibly difficult text to understand. The text also comes with visuals of Rama and Lakshmana. I would like the students to do a they say I say with the visual aids. I think this could be especially helpful in all culturally unfamiliar images. This is a great way to discuss multicultural art.

What, if anything, do you need help thinking through as you plan for using They Say / I Say in your classroom?:

I think I want to overcomplicate the they say/ I say. So I would say the only thing I need help with is giving myself permission to take the strategy and make it work for my class. Sometimes the I say can be as simple as having questions or paraphrasing or summarizing what the author said. It is like how I use they say I say when I do my own bible study. It is like a conversation with a text.

Name: Charity Bentley
Badge: Teacher as Researcher: Cultivating Literacy Routines
Looking back on this inquiry cycle, what did you learn about your teaching and your students that you might not have noticed otherwise? How did engaging in this teacher-research process shape or shift your thinking about your classroom practice?:

I learned it's very important to give my students opportunities to engage in high energy, but low stakes, writing. Talking to them about thinking "outside of the box" is as important in middle school as in high school. However, timed writing overwhelms some students, so it's very important to assure them that it's okay to simply write what they can. Having these "ingredient" handy for use in whatever activities we cook up along the road is very appealing. Knowing that I can modify the resources to suit virtually any subject or style makes them very valuable.

What new questions or next steps are you leaving this module with? How might you continue to explore or build on this inquiry in your future teaching?:

My next steps are to return to the Quicklists and Writing Sprints we did, but repeat them after the holidays have passed. I'm interested to see how students will feel about what they have already created, and wonder if they'll wish to change anything. Moving forward, I look forward to using the Say-Mean-Matter strategy as we dig into argumentative writing and the use of quality evidence. I do anticipate using the Quicklist and Writing Sprint tools for the argumentative unit, which will be especially easy now that student familiarity has been established. These approaches are easy to use and enjoyable, and that is exciting for teachers and students.

Looking back on this inquiry cycle, what did you learn about your teaching and your students that you might not have noticed otherwise? How did engaging in this teacher-research process shape or shift your thinking about your classroom practice?:

Planning is very intentional, knowing where children are, where they need to be, and how to close the gap is important. Even with the instructional activity quicklists and writing sprints, me working more to tweak my prompts for children was more important than putting the focus on them-- i change behavior and productivity of children by adjusting my approaches, requests, and instruction. I jumped right in with the kids after writing the 10-11 quick list prompts and realized- after I practiced it, I needed to alter or be more specific in my asking. They are trying, they are giving effort-- work to change my approach. In my notebook I altered prompts- and I saw it was more successful in bringing out the work in students. Setting aside specific planning and practicing prior is so key.

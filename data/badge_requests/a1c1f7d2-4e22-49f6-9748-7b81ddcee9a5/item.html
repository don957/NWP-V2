Badge: Reading to Write: They Say / I Say
Which version of the They Say / I Say routine (with visual texts or with annotation) feels like the best fit for your students right now? Why?:

The version of They Say/I say routine that best fits my students right now is with visual texts. I teach World History, and it is often hard for my students to understand different cultures around the world, so I use visuals as well as videos. For example, I teach about the eight features of a civilization. One feature of the civilization is writing. I show examples of cuneiform writing and the importance. I finish the lesson by allowing students to practice cuneiform writing and writing about their experience.

Describe one way you might adapt the They Say / I Say framework to align with the types of texts your students read most often (e.g., articles, visuals, multimodal texts).:

I would align this framework with visuals, articles, and movie clips. When students have a format, I will be able to see if they understand the lesson being taught.

What, if anything, do you need help thinking through as you plan for using They Say / I Say in your classroom?:

No.

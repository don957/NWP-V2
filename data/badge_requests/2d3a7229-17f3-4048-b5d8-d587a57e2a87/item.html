Badge: Teacher as Researcher | Sustaining Literacy Routines
Looking back on this inquiry cycle, what did you learn about your teaching and your students that you might not have noticed otherwise? How did engaging in this teacher-research process shape or shift your thinking about your classroom practice?:

I learned that my teaching is very much a trial and error style and I learn best after having tried a lesson multiple times. I noticed that my students were not use to taking time to write in short spurts about the same topic, but were able to engage positively through conversation about the topic when new information was presented. Engaging in this process shaped my classroom by making me think more about offering multiple perspectives for how society felt about important historical figures throughout history.

Think back to a moment during the lesson sequence that stands out. It could be a comment from a student, an unexpected challenge or success, a shift in engagement, or a moment when your own thinking changed. Describe what happened and why this moment feels meaningful to you now. What does it reveal:
A moment of meaning during this lesson occurred when a student began questioning their previous thoughts regarding daylight saving time and wondering what their new stance would be based on the evidence from the text. This revels how a simple text can move a student to make connections and draw their own conclusions.

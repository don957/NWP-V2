Looking back on this inquiry cycle, what did you learn about your teaching and your students that you might not have noticed otherwise? How did engaging in this teacher-research process shape or shift your thinking about your classroom practice?:

Engaging in this teacher-research process shifted my thinking about my classroom practice to realize that they don't always need these big/long elaborate text to write about and annotate. Sometimes, just something as simple as the title of an article or video can be enough to get a good discussion going! I learned that my students are more engaged when it is a topic that they are interested in. Or maybe I always knew that but with the content I teach and the curriculum I use, it is always feasible to have an article that they are interested in.

Think back to a moment during the lesson sequence that stands out. It could be a comment from a student, an unexpected challenge or success, a shift in engagement, or a moment when your own thinking changed. Describe what happened and why this moment feels meaningful to you now. What does it reveal:
One moment that stood out to me was when a student pointed out from the article that the article isn't against teens being on their phone but suggests better ways to manage screen time. This brought the class to a good discussion and it revealed participation, engagement, and debating. This moment feels meaningful to me now because it allowed that student to have that deeper thinking and thoughts on their own without needing to be guided there and then it opened up a world of opportunity for discussion!
As you look over the drafts your students produced in this lesson sequence, what early patterns, surprises, or questions are emerging for you? What aspects of your students’ writing are you most curious to explore more deeply with colleagues during our April regroup? What, if any, questions do you h:
As I look over my students’ drafts, I’m noticing that many of them can form a claim, but they struggle to clearly explain their reasoning and fully develop their ideas. I was a little surprised by how often students mentioned evidence without really connecting it back to their claim. I’m most curious to explore strategies for helping students strengthen their analysis and make clearer connections in their writing.

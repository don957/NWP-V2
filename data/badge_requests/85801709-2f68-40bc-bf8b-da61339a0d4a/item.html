Which version of the They Say / I Say routine (with visual texts or with annotation) feels like the best fit for your students right now? Why?:

I think I would use the texts and annotations more for my classroom because this is what we see on the EOC; however, I would like to try it with visuals too. I like that it prompts students to consider the meaning of the visual and not just recall what it is.

Describe one way you might adapt the They Say / I Say framework to align with the types of texts your students read most often (e.g., articles, visuals, multimodal texts).:

I would probably use the They Say / I Say framework for short films and theme. I like that it reframes themes in a way that watchers have to identify what the director is trying to communicate. It taps into author's purpose as well.

What, if anything, do you need help thinking through as you plan for using They Say / I Say in your classroom?:

I need help thinking through tying this activity to specific standards. I know this is a standards-based practice, but I want to dial it down to be specific.

Looking back on this inquiry cycle, what did you learn about your teaching and your students that you might not have noticed otherwise? How did engaging in this teacher-research process shape or shift your thinking about your classroom practice?:

I have learned that my students truly are able to be deeper thinkers and are ready to analyze texts in a deeper, more meaningful way. Are they perfect all of the time? Not yet, but we're working on being the best they can be! This process ensured me that while many of the things that "I've always done" are great for students, that it doesn't hurt to try something new or to give the students a new process or different way to think about things.

This students said that the say/mean/matter "felt weird" to her because she uses the strategy but putting it on paper and thinking about her thinking felt strange. She told me that while it was "weird", she appreciated it because it extended her thoughts about the quote farther than she would've thought on her own.

I had another student, who is one of my students that doesn't take as much time on his work as he could or should, make the comment that " this was a good strategy because it made me think deeper and I'm not normally a deep thinker". Knowing this student well, that comment made me chuckle.

What new questions or next steps are you leaving this module with? How might you continue to explore or build on this inquiry in your future teaching?:

I am going to put some more time into exploring the They say/I say strategy to help my students become more proficient users of the strategy and I will definitely continue using the say/mean/matter strategy so that it becomes more automatic for them. I would like to extend this into having them do more "formal" writing to with the strategy and have them explain their thought process and why they came up with their "matter" for whatever the quote may be.

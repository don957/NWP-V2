Looking back on this inquiry cycle, what did you learn about your teaching and your students that you might not have noticed otherwise? How did engaging in this teacher-research process shape or shift your thinking about your classroom practice?:

Looking back on this inquiry cycle, I realized that I was more intentional in my use of literacy strategies than I had been previously. Engaging in this process pushed me to slow down and thoughtfully plan how students would interact with texts, rather than assuming understanding would develop naturally. I also gained new insight into how my students engage with literacy tasks—particularly the level of scaffolding and guidance they need in order to successfully analyze and respond to content. Participating in the teacher-research process shifted my thinking about classroom practice by highlighting the importance of purposeful strategy selection and reflection. Rather than viewing literacy strategies as an add-on, I began to see them as an essential part of instruction that supports student comprehension and engagement across content areas. This cycle reinforced the value of reflective practice and using student responses to inform instructional decisions moving forward.

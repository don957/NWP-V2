Name: Stacey Ellison
Badge: Joining a Conversation
What is one insight you gained from completing the Atwoodian Table yourself that might shape how you introduce it to students?:

The hardest part was placing myself. I'm not sure students will have the same issue. As teachers we often times are forced to keep our personal opinions hidden and making a choice as I was interacting with the text was a challenge, I had to re-read a couple of times beause I forgot what my purpose was. I was much better at showing my opinion in my drawing of the others at the table and how I viewed their opinons "valid" or not. I think reminding students to keep info short will be key. Maybe limiting number of words they can use at the table for each person.

How does the Atwoodian Table idea compare to how you have taught argument and/or responding to multiple texts in the past?:
Completely new and unique. I love it! I like that it makes them see different perspectives not just theirs. It also neutralizes the topic, not I like or don't like or good or bad; just her are the facts.
What do you notice about the two sets of excerpts in this module (children in fancy restaurants and toddlers and technology)? How do the excerpts work together to invite students to join the conversation? What are some conversations related to topics you teach?:
I liked that all were short and relatable. I teach ELA so not so much on topics like that. We do argumentative writing and have been trying different ways the last few years looking for something that fits our students, this may be it!

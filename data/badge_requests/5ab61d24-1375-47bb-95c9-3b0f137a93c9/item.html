Name: Michelle Corbin
Badge: Joining a Conversation
What is one insight you gained from completing the Atwoodian Table yourself that might shape how you introduce it to students?:

Completing the Atwoodian Table myself was an important learning experience because it pushed me to slow down and truly engage with multiple perspectives before forming my own response. I know that I will have to emphasize to students that the goal is not to simply fill in each space, but to track their thinking in order to realize how different ideas are building upon one another.

How does the Atwoodian Table idea compare to how you have taught argument and/or responding to multiple texts in the past?:
When I compare this approach to how I have previously taught argument writing and multi-text responses, I realize that organizing thinking in this way gives students more of a visual of the idea that we all relate to different topics in different ways, and just because we feel our beliefs are the "right ones," that is not how thinking works. In the past, I have handed the kids different articles on paper - to either put back together in the correct sequence or to use in some kind of Kagan strategy. We would generally then work together through the article, breaking down, or highlighting the different parts of argumentative writing--claim, reason, evidence, bridge, etc. The articles were used more like different sources with the same topic, yet without the idea that these authors were participants in a conversation. This approach encourages more authentic thinking, as students are not just proving a point but developing ideas in response to a range of viewpoints.
What do you notice about the two sets of excerpts in this module (children in fancy restaurants and toddlers and technology)? How do the excerpts work together to invite students to join the conversation? What are some conversations related to topics you teach?:
These two texts working together are so engaging to students. The topics are things they know a little about, but then they can also learn so much more from others through their conversations as we go. Some of them act as experts in areas such as these. Opinions can easily be changed through the conversations that take place across a classroom. I just have to get them to write more of their thinking rather than them wanting to spout things out loud and then forgetting what they said. I need to be more intentional about selecting texts that work as well as these that "speak" to one another. In the past, one of my favorite topics for argumentative writing was wooden bats vs. metal bats. Kids this age have a lot to say about those!

Looking back on this inquiry cycle, what did you learn about your teaching and your students that you might not have noticed otherwise? How did engaging in this teacher-research process shape or shift your thinking about your classroom practice?:

I learned that I need structure and so do my students. I provided them minimal directions for this assignment and while I feel that they handled it, I think I could make some changes to ensure more success next time. In the future, I would like to set this up as a packet. I would begin by asking them to brainstorm about the topic. Then I would provide one text and follow it up with a question/quick write. Next, I would provide another text followed by question/quick write. This could be repeated with 2-4 texts. Then, I would have them wrap up by writing an entire essay to explain how their thoughts evolved and changed throughout the lesson.

Think back to a moment during the lesson sequence that stands out. It could be a comment from a student, an unexpected challenge or success, a shift in engagement, or a moment when your own thinking changed. Describe what happened and why this moment feels meaningful to you now. What does it reveal:
There was a shift in engagement from some students after we began. Some students were not thrilled when I handed out the first text. They immediately decided that it was "too hard". However, once they started reading the text and discussing with groups they began to show more interested and become more engaged. It makes such a difference when students enjoy what they are working on. It shows that they are becoming open-minded and are learning willingly.

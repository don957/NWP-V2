Badge: Reading to Write: They Say / I Say
Which version of the They Say / I Say routine (with visual texts or with annotation) feels like the best fit for your students right now? Why?:

I would use visual texts for my students. This method will reduce language load while clearly showing how ideas connect. Using sentences that are color-coded will allow students to see the difference between their own thinking and evidence-based responses. This supports comprehension, organization, and sentence construction—especially for students with language or processing needs.

Describe one way you might adapt the They Say / I Say framework to align with the types of texts your students read most often (e.g., articles, visuals, multimodal texts).:

Pairing visual text with explicit modeling, students can more easily transfer ideas from discussion or quick lists into complete sentences and paragraphs. The consistent visual structure builds independence, supports daily writing routines, and increases student confidence in using academic language.

What, if anything, do you need help thinking through as you plan for using They Say / I Say in your classroom?:

AS I plan for using this in my classroom, I think I would need help on finding the best method to use. Whether it is graphs, color coded, or another method .

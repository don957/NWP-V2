Name: Katherine DeBoer
Badge: Layering Reading, Writing and Thinking
How does the Layering Reading, Writing, and Discussion approach compare to how you have previously supported students in engaging with multiple texts or teaching argument writing? How might this process shift or refine the way you guide students toward forming their own claims?:

Previously, I did not use a layered approach when teaching argumentative writing. Typically, I would introduce a topic and then quickly ask students to choose a side. After that, they were asked to find evidence to support the claim they had already made. Looking back, I can see that this approach often pushed students to rush into forming a claim before they had fully informed themselves about the issue.

The Layering Reading, Writing, and Discussion approach significantly shifts that process. Instead of starting with a claim, students first engage with multiple perspectives through reading, writing, and discussion. This allows them to gather information, consider different viewpoints, and challenge their initial beliefs before deciding where they stand.

Using this process will refine the way I guide students toward forming their own claims because it encourages deeper thinking and a more informed argument. Rather than defending a position they chose quickly, students will have the opportunity to develop claims that are based on a stronger understanding of the topic and the evidence surrounding it.

How would you describe the role of talk and discussion in your classroom right now? What kinds of opportunities do students have to rehearse ideas through conversation? What challenges have you encountered in making talk a meaningful part of the writing process?:
Right now, talk and discussion play an important but sometimes uneven role in my classroom. Students do have opportunities to share and rehearse their ideas through conversations with peers, but not all students feel comfortable speaking up. I’ve noticed that some students tend to dominate the conversation, which can limit the participation of others. One challenge I’ve encountered is pacing—I often need to slow down and give students more time to process ideas and engage in meaningful discussion. Moving forward, I want to create more structured opportunities that allow all students to practice articulating their thoughts before they put them into writing.

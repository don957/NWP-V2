Looking back on this inquiry cycle, what did you learn about your teaching and your students that you might not have noticed otherwise? How did engaging in this teacher-research process shape or shift your thinking about your classroom practice?:

I’ve learned just how stagnant my teaching has become, which is something I have always feared. It’s been nice trying new things and stepping out of my comfort zone. I’ve also realized just how much I’ve been “teaching to the test,” and how awful and boring that must be for my students. Honestly, most days, teaching has become boring for me as well. There have been many lessons throughout this process that have felt refreshing, and I have already worked some of them into my units for next year.

Think back to a moment during the lesson sequence that stands out. It could be a comment from a student, an unexpected challenge or success, a shift in engagement, or a moment when your own thinking changed. Describe what happened and why this moment feels meaningful to you now. What does it reveal:
I have always prided myself on being able to put myself in my students' shoes and not forget how it felt to be that age (13-14). Still, when my students started discussing their stress with school start times and the many things they are expected to accomplish throughout the day, I was astonished that I couldn’t put myself in their shoes. My thinking changed here, and while I do my best to create units and lessons that are not only relevant to my students' situations but also engaging and interesting, when we began reading and talking about all things related to their sleep, I was amazed at how deep their conversations went. I’m thinking about polling my students every year to see where their interests lie, and I can adjust, as much as possible, when needed.

I’ve said this a million times already in other reflections, but having the students draw their thinking after reading/watching and taking notes was amazing. I’m not sure if the majority of students are now big visual learners due to screens, but students having a visual of their thinking process, for them and me, helped them comprehend the texts better. It was also great because my students were able to put themselves into the text easily with a drawing. I think that creating a more connected feeling to the text. After trying this out with my students, I’ve decided I am going to incorporate more visuals as we work through our units next year.

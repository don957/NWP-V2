Looking back on this inquiry cycle, what did you learn about your teaching and your students that you might not have noticed otherwise? How did engaging in this teacher-research process shape or shift your thinking about your classroom practice?:

I did work several years ago with a teacher in my school. I was her instructional coach. All of my teachers received the layered reading and writing training. I helped to give that training--though it was source-based argument work, and went beyond the work of this module. I had taken the training earlier and helped present this training to my teachers.

Recently, I also did a 1/2 day training session with teachers in the Rockwood school district. We took them through this process.

In both instances, teachers experienced a paradigm shift, as did I. Academic writing is a process of reading, talking, and writing. It is an organic, slippery thing that cannot be fully regimented. We needed to get used to the messiness of real writring.

Fromt the work I did with teachers in my school, the thing that sticks with me is not only the pride of the students--whose work was meaningful because of the mental investment--but also the pride of the teacher who also invested a lot. At first, she focused on the things students didn't do in the paper. But, when she focused on what they COULD do, her perspective shifted.

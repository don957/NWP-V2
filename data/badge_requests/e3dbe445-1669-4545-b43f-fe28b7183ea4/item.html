Name: Michelle Corbin
Badge: Reading to Write: They Say / I Say
Which version of the They Say / I Say routine (with visual texts or with annotation) feels like the best fit for your students right now? Why?:

I believe that we will have to start with the visual texts. Since we are at the beginning of a unit about the Holocaust - and preparing to work through some station work using two novels- I am going to use the They Say/I Say strategy with the use of a short video I like that outlines the life of Anne Frank and her diary. It is simple enough to help students get an idea of what the Holocaust really was. It also has photos that we can stop on during the showing/along with text describing the photo, which will be helpful when trying to get the students to write about their thinking through this strategy.

Describe one way you might adapt the They Say / I Say framework to align with the types of texts your students read most often (e.g., articles, visuals, multimodal texts).:

Two of the classes I have each day for 6th-grade ELA are co-teaching classrooms. About 1/3 of the students in each of these two hours have IEPs. My co-teacher and I always have to modify everything for some of those students, and we are always doing that in different ways. In writing, we usually write beginning sentence prompts in order to get more thinking from those kids. We also use a lot of fill-in-the-blank type worksheets with those students. We will have to use the same approach for this.

What, if anything, do you need help thinking through as you plan for using They Say / I Say in your classroom?:

As I plan to use the They Say / I Say strategy in my 6th grade ELA classroom, I need to think carefully about how to modify the strategy for students with IEPs, especially when working with a complex and emotionally heavy topic like the Holocaust, without taking away from this approach. Sentence starters and examples will definitely help with that.

Using the Anne Frank video, I will also need to consider processing time and emotional support, since students may react strongly to the content. For students who need it, I may allow oral responses, partner discussion, or drawing before writing. I will also chunk the task into smaller steps—first identifying what “they say” from the video, then focusing on one clear personal reaction for the “I say” portion.

Name: Debbie Ross
Badge: Layering Reading, Writing and Thinking
How does the Layering Reading, Writing, and Discussion approach compare to how you have previously supported students in engaging with multiple texts or teaching argument writing? How might this process shift or refine the way you guide students toward forming their own claims?:

I have taught writing for many years, and I have always had my students make a claim and then work to valid the claim they have made. The layering process and the research over various genres enables students to really search out a claim, maybe one they never thought they would like to delve into, and research. It also provides students with the opportunity to really look at an issue and decide if they are fully committed to the topic or have they changed their mind based on research. By annotating the sources which have been looked at will allow students to already have a working reference page from which to draw insight when writing the final copy of the paper. I think providing students with various types of genres to read and glean information will help my students become better rounded writers and really understand the writing process overall.

How would you describe the role of talk and discussion in your classroom right now? What kinds of opportunities do students have to rehearse ideas through conversation? What challenges have you encountered in making talk a meaningful part of the writing process?:

I believe some of my students may be reluctant at first to share with a classmate or me, but with practice, this process can become a regular routine in the wiriting process.

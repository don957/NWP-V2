Looking back on this inquiry cycle, what did you learn about your teaching and your students that you might not have noticed otherwise? How did engaging in this teacher-research process shape or shift your thinking about your classroom practice?:

It was less something I learned but something I was reminded of. Students are smarter than we give them credit for sometimes. When we don't feed them the information, but instead let them discover it for themselves, something wonderful comes of it. I know I want to incorporate more of student-led inquiry and discussion into the class.

One of my weakest writers, that has struggled this whole year with writing and engaging text, was able to use this process and come up with a claim with evidence. This was not something I saw before with him, so I was really proud when I read his work.
As you look over the drafts your students produced in this lesson sequence, what early patterns, surprises, or questions are emerging for you? What aspects of your students’ writing are you most curious to explore more deeply with colleagues during our April regroup? What, if any, questions do you h:
I noticed that some students were really good at finding relevant evidence to support their claims, while others struggled with that part of the assignment. I also really liked seeing them using techniques we'd covered earlier in the year to create a well-written essay. The thing I was most curious to see was how some of them were able to acknowledge the other side of the argument, while still sticking to their own side.

Name: Angie Kelim
Badge: Reading to Write: They Say / I Say
Which version of the They Say / I Say routine (with visual texts or with annotation) feels like the best fit for your students right now? Why?:

I believe it would be best to begin with They Say / I Say using visual texts. Many of our students are testing below grade level in reading (some of them being multiple grades below), so I think visual texts would go much further with them; they’d be better able to pull meaning from pictures than from something with a lot of text that they may not understand.

Describe one way you might adapt the They Say / I Say framework to align with the types of texts your students read most often (e.g., articles, visuals, multimodal texts).:

One adaptation I plan to make is to use visual texts to kick off a new unit or lesson, and perhaps have a small version of the photo available to students to tape into their writer’s notebook when we do the KWL to help remind them of exactly what we’re studying. They can refer back to the photos often and come up with additional questions or write in answers to questions they’ve already asked.

What, if anything, do you need help thinking through as you plan for using They Say / I Say in your classroom?:

I struggle to get information into a format or at a level that a majority of our students can understand, largely because of their low reading and math scores; reading comprehension and math abilities are both pretty important for scientific studies. We also have several students who, perhaps because of the deficits, have simply decided that they don’t care and they won’t put forth any real effort. How do you get junior high or high school students to start caring about school again?

Name: Lindsey McMillan
Badge: Reading to Write: They Say / I Say
Which version of the They Say / I Say routine (with visual texts or with annotation) feels like the best fit for your students right now? Why?:

I think using images for They Say/I Say will work well for most of my 8th graders, especially my multilingual learners and students with IEPs. I don’t typically use images with writing prompts, but pairing the They Say/I Say method with an image gives a new meaning to “writing prompt" for me. I think images will work well with my students because they often struggle with writing.

I recently used images to begin our Holocaust unit and had students write down what they noticed in the image. This was very impactful for other students and me. Without prompting, I found my students adding adjectives to their observations instead of just describing them. Without knowing it at the time, I believe that was very much a They Say/I Say thing to do. I am excited to use more images with this method, but I also want to try using this method in our nonfiction article annotation unit we do at the beginning of the year.

Describe one way you might adapt the They Say / I Say framework to align with the types of texts your students read most often (e.g., articles, visuals, multimodal texts).:

As I said above, I want to use the They Say/I Say method with nonfiction articles as well. We already touched on this when we worked with CER on our article, but I think it would be interesting to try this method out by having students read our local newspaper. This would be a great introduction to the method, and get students excited and interested in things happening local to them, and find that they do have something to say about it. Using the local paper, I would probably give students a choice board and let them choose an image with a caption, an article, a recipe, sports news, etc. I know my students have a lot more to say when they get to choose something they are interested in.

What, if anything, do you need help thinking through as you plan for using They Say / I Say in your classroom?:

I’m not sure if I need anything at the moment to help me through this process. Often, I find my students lack the motivation to share their opinion on something we read. I do have a hard time finding articles and images appropriate for middle school, so maybe some help locating the best resources for my students would be nice.

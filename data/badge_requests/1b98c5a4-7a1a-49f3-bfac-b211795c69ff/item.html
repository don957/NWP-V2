Which strategy from this module (Writer’s Notebook, Quicklists, or Writing Sprints) feels most immediately usable in your teaching context? Why?:

I’m looking forward to using all of the strategies from the module throughout this school year. The one I plan to use most quickly is the quicklist strategy. It lends itself to multiple lessons and units of study, and I believe can be easily incorporated as a bellringer. Because our class times seems to fly by, streamlined are definitely preferable; those that take relatively little time, but yield significant results and/or materials (so to speak) are greatly appreciated.

Quicklists are very practical, and fit perfectly into brainstorming and early planning stages of many writing assignments, so I anticipate utilizing them several times this year. While I think it’s very important to use varying approaches to writing, I also believe that there is value in establishing comfort and familiarity with some practices, because students need to know what is expected of them.

What do you anticipate will be the biggest challenge in bringing daily writing into your classroom, and how might you address it?:

The challenges to implementing the quicklist strategy are likely to be the same we encounter with most lessons. Students often fail to focus on instructions given for specific tasks, and then ask and reask questions. Probably one of the most difficult challenges is finding a way to help students think “outside the box.” In my experience, this is an ongoing battle.

Name: Katherine DeBoer
Badge: Joining a Conversation
What is one insight you gained from completing the Atwoodian Table yourself that might shape how you introduce it to students?:

I admit that I am a reluctant writer and often feel intimidated to put my thoughts down on paper. I found that I actually enjoyed this activity and felt comfortable participating. I can imagine that this structure and metaphor, taking a seat at the table, will also help my reluctant writers. These types of activities are best introduced by walking through an example together as a class. The students need to see the idea from start to finish and to hear conversations from their classmates before taking it on independently.

How does the Atwoodian Table idea compare to how you have taught argument and/or responding to multiple texts in the past?:
The Atwoodian Table is a much less intimidating way to introduce argumentative writing. The excerpts were short and clear, as opposed to several lengthy articles to work through.
What do you notice about the two sets of excerpts in this module (children in fancy restaurants and toddlers and technology)? How do the excerpts work together to invite students to join the conversation? What are some conversations related to topics you teach?:
I noticed there were multiple nuanced responses as opposed to one clear pro and one clear con response. The excerpts work together by providing an opportunity to agree, disagree, and jump in to put your own spin on the thoughts that have already been put on the table. My class recently completed a DBQ on the pros and cons of the Great Wall of China. I can see this being a topic for discussion.

What is one insight you gained from completing the Atwoodian Table yourself that might shape how you introduce it to students?:

The biggest insight for me when using the Atwoodian Table was being able to look at multiple viewpoints and notice how each viewpoint altered my thinking, even if just a little. It was nice to see all sides and know there are probably even more sides to this conversation that would alter my viewpoint yet again. When I use this strategy with my students, I hope they have similar effects. So many times when we research, my students want to focus on their opinion or side instead of considering any other positions, even when I give/have them look at various articles or websites. I'm pretty honest with my students, so when I introduce this strategy with them, I'll show them my table and how I went into each reading as open-minded as I could. They know how I feel about technology, so I hope they will understand that they, too, might be able to go into our argument readings with an open mind. Even if they don't change their minds about their opinion after reading multiple perspectives, I hope they can at least acknowledge other sides and see the benefit in doing so.

How does the Atwoodian Table idea compare to how you have taught argument and/or responding to multiple texts in the past?:
I have always taught students how to write a research questions before reading for the information. I teach 8th grade, so I believe I will still need to give some kind of guidance when we read articles for research, but I also want to loosen up on having a set question in place before allowing student to conduct their research. I don't really think I can compare my approach to the Atwoodian Table at all because it is such a different approach.
What do you notice about the two sets of excerpts in this module (children in fancy restaurants and toddlers and technology)? How do the excerpts work together to invite students to join the conversation? What are some conversations related to topics you teach?:
I know my students would love to join the "adult table" and both sets of excerpts does a good job sharing a variety of voices. In doing that students feel like there's space for them too. With these specific excerpts being about younger children, I know my students would feel both connected to the subject matter and also feel like they might have an expert opinion because they are still kids too. My students will being working on their argument essay soon and will argue about school start times or risks involved in contact sports. A lot of the conversations we've had so far, regarding school start time is "adult not understanding or caring about their perspective." My students really want the adults in charge to listen to their take on everything school related and I'm hoping they will feel they get to voice their thoughts and concerns, and be taken seriously, when I present the Atwoodian table to them.

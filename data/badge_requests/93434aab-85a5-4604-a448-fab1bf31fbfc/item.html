Badge: Teacher as Researcher: Cultivating Literacy Routines
Looking back on this inquiry cycle, what did you learn about your teaching and your students that you might not have noticed otherwise? How did engaging in this teacher-research process shape or shift your thinking about your classroom practice?:

I entered this process thinking that the They Say/I Say strategy would naturally lead to deeper thinking by my students. This inquiry cycle showed me that students were not prepared to make connections between the quotes I picked from the Declaration of Independence and the prior events we studied that lead to this moment in history, such as the conflict over taxation. I was hoping to see deeper thinking in my students' responses; however, most were simplistic. Analyzing student writing helped me to see the gap between comprehending the text and historical thinking skills. I now realize that I need to do a better job scaffolding the text not just for comprehension, but for the historical context necessary for students interpret primary sources meaningfully. After completing this process, I can see that I was more focused on teaching the strategy, than teaching the skill of analyzing. In the future, I will be more intentional with modeling how They Say/I Say can be used to make deeper analysis of text by making connections to other events and concepts taught in class by doing Think Alouds. Additionally, students may need targeted practice on contextualizing sources before they read independently.

What new questions or next steps are you leaving this module with? How might you continue to explore or build on this inquiry in your future teaching?:

As I look forward to teaching the creation of the Constitution, I can see another opportunity to use the They Say/I Say strategy while students learn about the Federalist and anti-Federalists. This time I will be more intentional in building students up so that they can use historical context to better demonstrate historical thinking in their written responses.

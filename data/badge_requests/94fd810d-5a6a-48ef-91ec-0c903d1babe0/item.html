Badge: Joining a Conversation
What is one insight you gained from completing the Atwoodian Table yourself that might shape how you introduce it to students?:

When I put the source on the table, I also noted the role. I would want to emphasize that point with students. This person works for which company? How does that company make money? Oh! As my students are listening to the conversation, I would also want them to have an inner monologue: Who is this person? What motivates their thinking? What is their expertise? Before they formulate their thinking—which is hopefully informed by the sources—they are thinking not only about the messages but also about credibility.

How does the Atwoodian Table idea compare to how you have taught argument and/or responding to multiple texts in the past?:
Way back in the day I was guilty of giving kids a pro/con article and asking them pick a side and explain their thinking using the pros or cons from that one article. Ugh. I was trained in C3WP when NWP was developing it. It was a paradigm shift for me. I've been spreading the good word ever since.
What do you notice about the two sets of excerpts in this module (children in fancy restaurants and toddlers and technology)? How do the excerpts work together to invite students to join the conversation? What are some conversations related to topics you teach?:
Variety of perspectives (not just simple pros or cons). At least one sources is highly reputable. One source--while not being uncredible--has a definitely bias to account for. Then, you should leave out at least one voice so that students can figure out who is NOT at the table. The excerpts present detailed enough information so that students can reason from evidence. We used a set of excerpts on AI in our last PD.

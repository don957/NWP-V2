Badge: Reading to Write: They Say / I Say
Which version of the They Say / I Say routine (with visual texts or with annotation) feels like the best fit for your students right now? Why?:

Right now, I think the best fit for my students would be the online annotation process where the students can comment on one another's posts. I frequently annotate in my classroom on hard copy articles but sometimes the kids don't know how to get started or know how to find the 'important' information. I think giving them They Say/I Say templates is a great way to take off some of that pressure. I also think being able to connect with one other and comment back and forth, without the pressure of speaking in person, is a great bonus. I don't think this replaces face to face interactions and responses but adds a level of confidence for creating quality discussions.

Describe one way you might adapt the They Say / I Say framework to align with the types of texts your students read most often (e.g., articles, visuals, multimodal texts).:

We read articles consistently in my classroom and I plan to adapt this technique to every class. Normally, I focus on one main nonfiction article per unit. This is a great strategy to use as a way to refer back to a text (nonfiction practice) or to have students comment both for pre-reading and post-reading. This would allow for us to revisit the text and see/discuss previous comments and watch how our perception of a topic may change over the course of a unit.

What, if anything, do you need help thinking through as you plan for using They Say / I Say in your classroom?:

I feel pretty confident in using this strategy. I would probably spend time thinking through some of the templates and possibly adding some or adjusting them to fit my specific needs.

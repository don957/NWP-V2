Name: Carissa Schroer
Badge: Teacher as Researcher: Cultivating Literacy Routines
Looking back on this inquiry cycle, what did you learn about your teaching and your students that you might not have noticed otherwise? How did engaging in this teacher-research process shape or shift your thinking about your classroom practice?:

I had noticed that CER was challenging for several students. When I began using the Say Mean Matter routine, I noticed that the CER answers got stronger. I think that students genuinely did not understand the reasoning piece of CER and I mistook it as cutting a corner. I think the whole process gave me insight into how to model deeper thinking more clearly for student success. It also put a twist on CER to begin with the quote instead of beginning with the claim.

Share one artifact or anecdote from trying out your chosen strategy (e.g., a piece of student work, notes from your Writer’s Notebook, a classroom snapshot, or a brief story from the experience).:
What new questions or next steps are you leaving this module with? How might you continue to explore or build on this inquiry in your future teaching?:

I am planning for next year with the idea in mind that I want to do more modeling of the thinking aspect of the answers. I expected students to automatically do the thinking but they need to be explicitly led through with modeling and gradual release. When I begin next year, I will use Say Mean Matter and Writer's Notebooks, and other routines I've learned with Literacy That Works to lead them through the expected analysis and responses. In some ways, I can't wait until next year to start the year with these ideas!

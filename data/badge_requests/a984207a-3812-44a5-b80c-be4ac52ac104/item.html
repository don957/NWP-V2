Badge: Joining a Conversation
What is one insight you gained from completing the Atwoodian Table yourself that might shape how you introduce it to students?:

I would make sure that they are summarizing the main ideas. I think I like the idea of giving them 1 text at a time so I would definitely explain that we will be adding multiple voices. I go back and forth about telling them at the beginning that they need to think of their own claim/ "feelings" because they will be adding themselves. I think it would work well to not tell them because they will hopefully form their own opinion throughout reading without having to bring it up at all.

How does the Atwoodian Table idea compare to how you have taught argument and/or responding to multiple texts in the past?:
I think before (or at least when I was in school) I would make a claim or opinion and find texts to support it. I like that this works backwards and gives them the texts, makes them find the main ideas, and then make their own claim after they have read everything. I think that even though I would already have a claim in my mind beforehand sometimes it would change after I found texts and read more.
What do you notice about the two sets of excerpts in this module (children in fancy restaurants and toddlers and technology)? How do the excerpts work together to invite students to join the conversation? What are some conversations related to topics you teach?:
Both set of texts give multiple perspectives not just the "good" or the "bad". The students get to see both sides and multiple claims/opinions so that they can then join the conversation and either agree or disagree with claims that were made in the texts. Some conversations related to topics I teach would be genetic testing, how we are contributing to environmental change (are we bad/good for the environment), lab animals, etc.

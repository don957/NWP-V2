Name: Adam Rich
Badge: Layering Reading, Writing and Thinking
How does the Layering Reading, Writing, and Discussion approach compare to how you have previously supported students in engaging with multiple texts or teaching argument writing? How might this process shift or refine the way you guide students toward forming their own claims?:

I have seen this process used before and immediately understood its power to engage students in deeper thinking on a topic. Currently, I frequently use turn and talk or partner work in conjunction with reading or writing tasks. However, they are often surface level. For example, I might have students do a quick write, then have them pair and share. What I am lacking is the intentional layer of different perspectives. This model, along with the example lesson in January, have show me HOW to layer reading, writing, and discussion in a more purposeful way. I have been exploring text sets for the last couple of years. This module is a great model for how to effectively introduce each text in a way that promotes reflective writing that can help student trace the evolution of their opinions on a topic in response to new evidence. As a history teacher, this fits with my desire to help students understand the argumentative nature of academic history that is too often obscured by textbooks.

How would you describe the role of talk and discussion in your classroom right now? What kinds of opportunities do students have to rehearse ideas through conversation? What challenges have you encountered in making talk a meaningful part of the writing process?:
In classroom, student turn and talk or stand up/pair up multiple times during class period. As I stated above, these encounters often only require surface level thinking. While it does provide a chance to rehearse their ideas, I'm not able to prove it has a meaningful impact on their writing. The challenge is to make the conversation purposeful. I tried to float around the room and listen, but there is only so much I can monitor at once.

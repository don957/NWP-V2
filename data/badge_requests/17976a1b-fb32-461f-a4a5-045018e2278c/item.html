Name: Haley Murray
Badge: Everyday Sparks
Which strategy from this module (Writer’s Notebook, Quicklists, or Writing Sprints) feels most immediately usable in your teaching context? Why?:

I plan on using both quick lists and writing sprints. I think both techniques would be great in all of my ELA classes. For my ENG II and 8th grade ELA classes, we focus a lot on historical contexts. The quick lists would be a great way to add additional information to my units. For example, I could change the quick write topics from: Things I Did Yesterday to Things that Remind Me of the 1920's to add context to my Gatsby unit. These are immediate and low pre strategies that can be used as bell work, station work, independent work, or an exit ticket.

What do you anticipate will be the biggest challenge in bringing daily writing into your classroom, and how might you address it?:

I think the biggest challenge is the creative portion. Sometimes students struggle with the ability to confidently come up with ideas on the spot. To address this, I can make sure that I give specific instructions and specific prompts. As the students get used to the quick lists or writing sprints, I could broaden the topics and hope for more creativity authenticity. I think the best way to grow confidence in writing is to give students the time they need to grow their skills.

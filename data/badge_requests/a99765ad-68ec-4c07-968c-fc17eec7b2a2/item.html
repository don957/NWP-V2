Name: Cathy Rahder
Badge: Reading to Write: They Say / I Say
Which version of the They Say / I Say routine (with visual texts or with annotation) feels like the best fit for your students right now? Why?:

I use this They Say/I Say with both visual and writing. I use it much more with writing; however, I will now use the title "They Say/I Say" and explain what that means to my students. Since reading comprehension seems to be on the decline in our country, I feel that emphasizing it with writing is crucial. I always hear students say they hate to read. It makes me sad. I want them to love reading, but I think it starts with loving to write and understanding how the two connect.

Describe one way you might adapt the They Say / I Say framework to align with the types of texts your students read most often (e.g., articles, visuals, multimodal texts).:

Again, since I use this daily, I can describe what my lesson plan looks like. I use short stories as well as novels to teach my concepts. Let's take, for instance, when I taught Author's Purpose. I used the short story "The Story of an Hour" (6th-grade adaptation). We tore that text apart. We read it once through with no annotations, just listening. The kids seemed confused about its content. However, over the next couple of weeks, we read it over and over. Each time, focusing on a single concept. Once was for the author's word choice. I asked, "Why did the author use this word?" What if she used "this word" instead? How would the meaning change? Another time was for character analysis. We tore the main character apart. Why did she do this or that? What "traits" go along with her actions? Next, we focused on summarization. We took each paragraph, and I asked them to summarize it in ten words or fewer. They love this one. My kids now ask for paper to annotate text all the time without me even asking.

What, if anything, do you need help thinking through as you plan for using They Say / I Say in your classroom?:

I actually don't need any help with this process in my classroom. I love this process.

What is one insight you gained from completing the Atwoodian Table yourself that might shape how you introduce it to students?:

One insight I gained is that knowledge and beliefs about a topic don't always come from facts, but experience. It was easy to see when all the ideas were laid out side by side. This would be a great conversation to have with students that the evidence they collect doesn't have to come from statistics or data. Instead it can come from experience by a person. I would like to teach students to use this as a perspective-building tool. They can use this model to help them develop their own perspective.

How does the Atwoodian Table idea compare to how you have taught argument and/or responding to multiple texts in the past?:
Previously, when teaching argument, I’ve often structured lessons around claim, evidence, counterclaim, rebuttal. Students would read two texts, identify each author’s position, and then respond—usually moving fairly quickly toward choosing a side. While that approach works, it sometimes leads students to treat the opposing view as something to “defeat” rather than understand.
What do you notice about the two sets of excerpts in this module (children in fancy restaurants and toddlers and technology)? How do the excerpts work together to invite students to join the conversation? What are some conversations related to topics you teach?:
Both modules have students find information about what others are saying about the topic. They are introduced to viewpoints on all sides of the topic. Once students have the chance to see all the viewpoints about the topic, they are asked to join the conversation. Students are able to use the information to develop their own claim or ideas about the topic.

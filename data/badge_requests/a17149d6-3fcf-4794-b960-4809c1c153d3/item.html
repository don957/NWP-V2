Name: Breanna Smith
Badge: Layering Reading, Writing and Thinking
How does the Layering Reading, Writing, and Discussion approach compare to how you have previously supported students in engaging with multiple texts or teaching argument writing? How might this process shift or refine the way you guide students toward forming their own claims?:

I often had students read a text, a few sources, or even just a topic strip and then move fairly quickly into a written response or argument. When I would attempt to have discussion, it wasn't always structured. With layering the process feels more intentional because students run through reading, informal writing, and discussion in stages. This gives them more time to process ideas from each text and think about the evidence before forming a claim. This process could refine how I guide students towards forming their own claims because it emphasizes on developing ideas gradually.

How would you describe the role of talk and discussion in your classroom right now? What kinds of opportunities do students have to rehearse ideas through conversation? What challenges have you encountered in making talk a meaningful part of the writing process?:
In my classroom, talk and discussion play an important role. We are always trying out new protocols with plenty of opportunities to get students up, moving, talking, and discussing. My students will meet with their clock partners after each questioned they answered and compare answers and discuss. If they both have the same (correct) answer, they discuss the evidence in the article that lead them to that answer. If they have different answers, they are to debate who is right and why, using evidence in the article to back them up.

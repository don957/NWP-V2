Badge: Joining a Conversation
What is one insight you gained from completing the Atwoodian Table yourself that might shape how you introduce it to students?:

The Atwoodian Table is probably my favorite strategy for writing so far in this program. I really did enjoy reading about the different points of view and then being able to come up with my own rationale after looking at various thinking. I feel like this is a very important skill for our students to develop sense there is so much information available to them that they will need to learn how to decipher and make their own decisions.

How does the Atwoodian Table idea compare to how you have taught argument and/or responding to multiple texts in the past?:
The Atwoodian Table gave the students an opportunity to look at a few good examples from other people's perspectives before asking them to come up with their own thoughts.
What do you notice about the two sets of excerpts in this module (children in fancy restaurants and toddlers and technology)? How do the excerpts work together to invite students to join the conversation? What are some conversations related to topics you teach?:
Both sets of excerpts were looking at younger children and what the students feel would be best for that age group within the two different situations. I think this would allow the students to feel like the "expert" on the topic and to remove themselves from the situation since it's not talking about topics directly affecting them. I teach math, so I think we could do this same activity around the concept of whether AI should be allowed to check their work or if that would be considered cheating.

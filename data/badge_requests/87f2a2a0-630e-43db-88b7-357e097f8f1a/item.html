Badge: Joining a Conversation
What is one insight you gained from completing the Atwoodian Table yourself that might shape how you introduce it to students?:

An insight that I gained is that you don't have to agree with everything you read, but it can help to develop your own position on a topic. I think that this will help students with differing opinions, and possibly those who just carry the opinions of their parents, see that it's okay to hold their own opinions about a topic, but still learn from others. I wrote in the discussion about Newton and Robert Hooke. I won't have time to develop this idea for my Forces and Interactions unit this year, but I want to figure out how to incorporate it into next year. Instead, I'm looking forward as I plan improvements to my next unit, Energy, to using the Atwoodian Table as part of my layered writing strategy.

How does the Atwoodian Table idea compare to how you have taught argument and/or responding to multiple texts in the past?:
I haven't ever taught argument before in 7th grade science. That's what makes it particularly exciting for me. I enjoy trying new things, and adapting the way I teach to reach as many learning styles as possible. It has piqued my interest.
What do you notice about the two sets of excerpts in this module (children in fancy restaurants and toddlers and technology)? How do the excerpts work together to invite students to join the conversation? What are some conversations related to topics you teach?:
I notice that both topics were presented with short texts that would be easily attainable by most students. They are both something with which they each have some personal experience, even if just a little bit. As they are reading, if we pre-load the reading with an explanation of what it means to 'join the conversation' they can be thinking about what they can say as they are reading. It's not intimidating to add a thought bubble to your own place at the table. That made me think, I could use one of these sets of excerpts to model how to use it. It could be used as a class opener the day after my unit test. This is me thinking out loud. 😄. With my Energy unit I plan to weave in a new to me unit called Human Impact, and I'd like to introduce differing stances on climate change. These are just beginning ideas, but could be very interesting.

Looking back on this inquiry cycle, what did you learn about your teaching and your students that you might not have noticed otherwise? How did engaging in this teacher-research process shape or shift your thinking about your classroom practice?:

I tried a layered learning activity in the fall with these classes, and it went well, but it was definitely rushed and didn't really connect to anything else we were doing. This time around, we took our time. Four days, and if I'm honest, it probably could go to five and be a more developed piece of writing, but we are also in EOC time, so I'm trying to get them used to doing all of this in a shorter period of time like on the test. I learned that my students still have some gaps in their synthesis and argument skills that we need to address. We've been doing a lot of practice with reading and responding to articles and recognizing the claim in the article, but not as much with synthesizing multiple texts or creating their own arguments/claims. I don't think I really had a shift here, it was just another great protocol to add to my arsenal and think about how and when it will fit in. But as I mentioned in my earlier post, I love that this gave us some language surrounding the academic conversation that is reading and writing. When we go into our choice driven research and inquiry project, I can reinforce this idea of a conversation that we are entering, exploring, and joining. I intend to keep the same framework and have them provide the three texts.

Think back to a moment during the lesson sequence that stands out. It could be a comment from a student, an unexpected challenge or success, a shift in engagement, or a moment when your own thinking changed. Describe what happened and why this moment feels meaningful to you now. What does it reveal:
At the end of class on lesson two, two boys were deeply involved in the conversation about school start time and stayed past the bell to confer with me. It is truly joyous to see their literacy expanding and see 15/16 year olds getting excited about the material in class. I'm not getting 100% buy in all the time with everything I do, but I've really been paying attention to the small wins lately, and just how much these kids have grown over this school year.

I think they look pretty standard. Some students are doing the bare minimum, some are doing outstanding. Some have solid arguments with lines of reasoning and integrated evidence from the sources (I'm very pleased to see many of them still using Say Mean Matter) and others have a vague claim that drifts off topic, doesn't utilize the sources, etc. I guess I'm curious to see if there were any resources (graphic organizers, outlines, or other revisions) that other teachers developed for scaffolding or optional help for students to use. I think I would develop some of this for future use. My reluctant students are still growing, they had some good claims and points, but still need to develop and integrate evidence.

Looking back on this inquiry cycle, what did you learn about your teaching and your students that you might not have noticed otherwise? How did engaging in this teacher-research process shape or shift your thinking about your classroom practice?:

After looking back on this inquiry cycle I realized that my students weren't ready to find all the research they needed to proceed with their expository writings (argumentative). This process allowed me to take the time to observe what they were doing and thinking. Engaging in this teacher-research process shifted my thinking about my classroom practice because now I will be taking the time to break the process down, so the students don't get overwhelmed.

Think back to a moment during the lesson sequence that stands out. It could be a comment from a student, an unexpected challenge or success, a shift in engagement, or a moment when your own thinking changed. Describe what happened and why this moment feels meaningful to you now. What does it reveal:
A bunch of moments stand out with one student. I think the topic of AI caught his interest, so he was more engaged with the lesson. Also, he is my reluctant writer, and breaking down the the process by layering helped him complete the work. He truly enjoyed bringing the three texts together by drawing a visual. It was meaningful because he has slowly been writing more. When I started working with him last year, I could hardly get one sentence out of him, and this layering process gave him the confidenence to turn in a 2 page paper.

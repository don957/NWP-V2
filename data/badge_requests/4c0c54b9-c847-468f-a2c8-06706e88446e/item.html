Looking back on this inquiry cycle, what did you learn about your teaching and your students that you might not have noticed otherwise? How did engaging in this teacher-research process shape or shift your thinking about your classroom practice?:

I learned that I sometimes underestimate certain students’ abilities. I was pleasantly surprised by the number of thoughtful and insightful responses. This process also reinforced the importance of allowing students to struggle productively, rather than stepping in too quickly to relieve minor discomfort. Giving them that space led to deeper thinking and stronger engagement.

Describe what happened and why this moment feels meaningful to you now. What does it reveal:
My last class period of the day is large and often quite energetic, and I can feel a bit frazzled by that point. During this lesson, however, we had a lively yet productive discussion after reading the article together. When students transitioned to independent writing, the room became completely silent, and they were writing intensely. When I asked them to begin wrapping up, several students asked for more time. This moment stood out to me because it revealed how invested they were in the discussion and how strongly they wanted their ideas to be heard. It shifted my thinking about this group and reminded me that even high-energy classes can engage deeply when the task is meaningful.

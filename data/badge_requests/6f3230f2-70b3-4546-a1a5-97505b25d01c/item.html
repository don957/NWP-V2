Badge: Reading to Write: They Say / I Say
Which version of the They Say / I Say routine (with visual texts or with annotation) feels like the best fit for your students right now? Why?:

I think a visual text might be the best fit for my students right now. In Biology, we deal with lots of visuals in the form of graphs, so being able to point out important parts of the graph and respond to them is a key skill.

Describe one way you might adapt the They Say / I Say framework to align with the types of texts your students read most often (e.g., articles, visuals, multimodal texts).:

I have seen an adaptation of this strategy before that was called "Tabletop Twitter". There were several stations set up with each station containing a piece of butcher paper with a phrase, text, or visual. During the first round, students had to respond to the visual silently by writing directly on the paper and moving on to the next station. In the second round, students responded again on the paper, but this time they responded to their peer's comments. I think this is a good adaptation to point out deficits in understanding, or reinforcing good points from their peers. Plus, this is all anonymous! Students do not fear judgement in this activity.

What, if anything, do you need help thinking through as you plan for using They Say / I Say in your classroom?:

I think I would need help thinking through how I would use this strategy when covering my Biology topics. I struggle to incorporate text sometimes (that isn't just informational about the concept/ vocabulary) so I'm not sure how I would incorporate multiple texts or pictures while also accomplishing staying on standard. I think a lot of these strategies are good when you're given the space to think creatively and openly; however, my Biology standards are pretty cut and dry.

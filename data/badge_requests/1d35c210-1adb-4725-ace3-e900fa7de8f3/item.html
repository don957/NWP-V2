Badge: Joining a Conversation
What is one insight you gained from completing the Atwoodian Table yourself that might shape how you introduce it to students?:

This routine really helped me to slow down and focus on each person's view. Doing it ahead of time helped me be better prepared for how I would present it to my small reading group. I knew what my students would be experiencing when they used this routine. I really enjoyed doing this with my students. They were surprisingly engaged. I think they enjoyed looking at the different opinions and then formulating their own.

How does the Atwoodian Table idea compare to how you have taught argument and/or responding to multiple texts in the past?:
I will be honest, my previous experience with argumentative writing was as a 2nd grade teacher. I did not have any experience working with middle schoolers on any type of writing. Just response to worksheets about their reading. I will say that going through the Aspire training, I have seen a lot of alignment with the routines/strategies we have been learning about. Atwoodian Table used more perspectives and with Aspire it was a lesson structure was hard to fit into my small reading groups. The Atwoodian Table idea, I was able to fit it into my reading groups better. The excerpts were quick and students were not overwhelmed with the reading.
What do you notice about the two sets of excerpts in this module (children in fancy restaurants and toddlers and technology)? How do the excerpts work together to invite students to join the conversation? What are some conversations related to topics you teach?:
I noticed that they gave multiple perspectives on both topics. The excerpts invited students in because they felt like they were part of the conversation. They "listened" to each person's views and then created their own opinion based on the input of others along with their previous thoughts and ideas.

Name: Katie Eden
Badge: Reading to Write: They Say / I Say
Which version of the They Say / I Say routine (with visual texts or with annotation) feels like the best fit for your students right now? Why?:

I enjoyed learning about both. Starting with visual texts would be beneficial for your struggling readers. They can tell you their thoughts and opinions without getting frustrated by the text. However, having the actual texts and being able to mark on them will help students cite text evidence and give reasoning for their thoughts and/or opinions.

Describe one way you might adapt the They Say / I Say framework to align with the types of texts your students read most often (e.g., articles, visuals, multimodal texts).:

Teaching students how to use the strategy with articles and stories would be a great way to show students how the strategy works first. I would then relate the strategy to online stories/articles. When students test on the computer, they don't have the printed text to mark on, so teaching note-taking strategies is important.

What, if anything, do you need help thinking through as you plan for using They Say / I Say in your classroom?:

Just being intentional with the text chosen to align with the standards being taught.

Looking back on this inquiry cycle, what did you learn about your teaching and your students that you might not have noticed otherwise? How did engaging in this teacher-research process shape or shift your thinking about your classroom practice?:

​I noticed that both me and my students do better when we have structure. The structured activities helped to guide classroom interactions (written and spoken). For example, the sentence stems took the pressure off of me when completing some of the tasks because it provided my blank mind a place to start so my own thoughts could flow. I also learned that is ok if the writing is not content focused. Sometimes we need to develop the whole student, not just the content. I can be intentional with the text I use and it does not always have to be a complete scientific article. And most importantly, students need the space, time, and scaffold to develop their view on topics. This requires me providing them with multiple perspectives, and the time and space to process them. Not only on their own, but through conversations with others. I think this will help them become more empathetic and less rigid in their daily life. It will also help them shape their individual thoughts from preconceived ones.

Describe what happened and why this moment feels meaningful to you now. What does it reveal:
​During some of the conversation portions of this lesson sequence, ​I was surprised by several students who took this activity seriously ​even though they were not getting a grade for it. One class in particular made my heart smile when they used appropriate academic language​ when supporting their claims using the text, then expanding on their thoughts with their own experiences.​ As a bonus, it looked like they were enjoying themselves. This revealed the importance of academic conversations in the learning process and reminded me of the reason I am doing this. Literacy is out of my comfort zone so seeing the benefits reminds me why I am invested in learning how to improve literacy in my classes. Especially when I feel overwhelmed or underqualified.
As you look over the drafts your students produced in this lesson sequence, what early patterns, surprises, or questions are emerging for you? What aspects of your students’ writing are you most curious to explore more deeply with colleagues during our April regroup? What, if any, questions do you h:
When looking over my student drafts, I am noticing that they need help with developing their own ideas in response to the source material. Most students just comment on how they feel about the evidence pulled from the text, and not actually expanding on the evidence. They can all make a basic claim though. I am curious on how other students relate their evidence and reasoning since mine struggle. I also want to see how they develop their reasoning. I am most excited to work with others to critique the arguments. I really do overthink this and as a result I struggle. I get so focused on treating this like an exemplar piece and giving them a grade, but more of a tool to use to help students see where they currently are and what their next step could be. Celebrating what they can do well and as well has direct them in what they can improve. Critiquing student writing and providing meaningful feedback is something I am not confident in doing. It was wonderful to be able to sit and discuss this with my colleagues. Honestly I wish we had more time for this during our session.

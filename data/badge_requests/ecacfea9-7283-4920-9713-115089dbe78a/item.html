Name: Isabella Orozco
Badge: Reading to Write: They Say / I Say
Which version of the They Say / I Say routine (with visual texts or with annotation) feels like the best fit for your students right now? Why?:

I used the visual texts in my instructional ELA classroom because my students are emerging readers with print related disabilities. This made practicing this skill much more attainable for them because it removes some of the barrier of reading. For many students in my class, this is their first year being expected to engage with a text beyond simple 5 W questions.

Describe one way you might adapt the They Say / I Say framework to align with the types of texts your students read most often (e.g., articles, visuals, multimodal texts).:

The way I adapted this was by scribing student responses for them at first, then we moved into using speech to text in order to practice independently.

What, if anything, do you need help thinking through as you plan for using They Say / I Say in your classroom?:

I do not think I need support at this time. The visual adaptation made this very easy to incorporate!

Badge: Teacher as Researcher: Cultivating Literacy Routines
Looking back on this inquiry cycle, what did you learn about your teaching and your students that you might not have noticed otherwise? How did engaging in this teacher-research process shape or shift your thinking about your classroom practice?:

I chose to use the Say Mean Matter strategy with a couple of my classes. While the first group didn't really have a chance to finish the activity in the time we had available, the other group did pretty well. I think it helped that they'd at least seen the strategy before with one of our other teachers. I began with a basic quote about working for what we want vs making excuses for not doing the things that we don't want to do; this seemed simple enough but still caused a couple of people to stumble when it came time to do the work. I'll be honest: we didn't do much modeling. I put the template on the board and I explained the process, but I didn't actually walk them through it. Perhaps I thought we didn't need to because I knew they'd seen it before, but looking back now I realize that I missed a good opportunity to help them succeed from the outset; even if we've done something previously it never hurts to review.

What new questions or next steps are you leaving this module with? How might you continue to explore or build on this inquiry in your future teaching?:

I'm wondering how I can get my students to move past the surface level stuff and truly *think* about what things mean and why it matters. Based on the examples I've seen so far, even students who seem to be "getting it" are just barely making a dent in the matter portion. Perhaps it's the selections I gave them, or maybe because it's still new....or maybe they're just accustomed to people not expecting more...regardless of the "why", it's still frustrating and I want to help them dig deeper. I definitely think this is a strategy we'll hang onto, and I'll use it for more than just passages of text.

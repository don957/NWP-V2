Name: Trisha Boyles
Badge: Reading to Write: They Say / I Say
Which version of the They Say / I Say routine (with visual texts or with annotation) feels like the best fit for your students right now? Why?:

I actually think this is perfect for my junior class that is preparing to do a mini-unit on propaganda. The They Say, I Say routine helps develop the thinking around what message is being conveyed and what do I think about that without students needing to struggle through dense text. Also, I think utilizing this with visuals first helps build the use of the strategy before students attempt to use it with texts.

Describe one way you might adapt the They Say / I Say framework to align with the types of texts your students read most often (e.g., articles, visuals, multimodal texts).:

In addition to using it with visuals/propaganda, this strategy works well with teaching students to analyze biased and argumentative text. I have used this in the past, and will continue to use it with my high school students. Further, I think it provides a great scaffold for struggling readers that have difficulties with knowing how to begin engaging with text. Sometimes, students struggle to just get started, and by giving them a direct line of thinking with sentence stems that provide response options, I think students are better equipped to work with texts they might otherwise ignore. Lastly, my students will mostly use this for articles and non-fiction texts in my classroom. They will use this to build their own responses to current events and biased news sources.

What, if anything, do you need help thinking through as you plan for using They Say / I Say in your classroom?:

I am familiar with this strategy as I taught dual credit courses several years ago and the text was used in those courses. I think it is a great way to help students learn that reading and writing is a conversation!

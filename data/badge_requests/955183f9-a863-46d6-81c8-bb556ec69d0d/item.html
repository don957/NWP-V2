Name: Angie Fulton
Badge: Everyday Sparks
Which strategy from this module (Writer’s Notebook, Quicklists, or Writing Sprints) feels most immediately usable in your teaching context? Why?:

I started a writers notebook with my biology 2 class. This is the class that I have the most freedom with so I thought it would be a good place to start. I actually completed the same Personal Narrative Quicklist that we did in the module. I did not want to start with a science based quicklist until both me and the students hand some practice with the strategy. I also decided to try this because it is a low stakes way to put thoughts and ideas to paper. This type of writing can be a struggle for me because my mind draws a blank. I assume I have students who feel the same way. I thought it would be a good place to start so I can grow with my students.

What do you anticipate will be the biggest challenge in bringing daily writing into your classroom, and how might you address it?:

I thought my biggest challenge would be student pushback since we were trying something new. I was pleasantly surprised that my students enjoyed the activity. Some didn't finish their lists, but they were frozen in thought. I chose three minutes for their first sprint and some felt like it was to long. The second sprint, I did a two minute sprint, and felt like it was too short, most of them felt like it was just right. I suspect that they will feel like this is not enough time with continued practice. Another challenge I anticipate is I often forget about great ideas, so actually remembering to implement this consistently is an area I will probably struggle with. Also, I used the same instructions that were in the module, so I will not upload that, I will however upload some student examples.

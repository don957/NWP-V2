Looking back on this inquiry cycle, what did you learn about your teaching and your students that you might not have noticed otherwise? How did engaging in this teacher-research process shape or shift your thinking about your classroom practice?:

Looking back, I learned that there are ways to differentiate for my students while using informational texts, and I learned the value of mixed-ability group discussions. I usually have the mindset of setting high expectations in order to push my advanced students and then me helping my struggling students on the side. This activity supported my struggling readers in a natural, supportive way without any extra prep!

Engaging in this teacher-research process shifted my classroom practice to be more collaboration-focused because my students did so well with it!

Think back to a moment during the lesson sequence that stands out. It could be a comment from a student, an unexpected challenge or success, a shift in engagement, or a moment when your own thinking changed. Describe what happened and why this moment feels meaningful to you now. What does it reveal:
A moment that stood out was when a boy who was very uninterested in the content (most likely because it was a bit outside of his comprehension level), exclaimed, "Ohhhh I get it now!" a few moments after our first discussion. This changed the trajectory of the rest of the lessons. He was engaged and confident throughout the rest of the lessons and by the end was even helping other students. This is meaningful to me because not only did it allow him to participate at a higher level than he normally does, but it also boosted his confidence when he was able to help a peer who was confused on what to do. I could see the pride in his demeanor!

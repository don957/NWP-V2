Name: Katie Cornine
Badge: Reading to Write: They Say / I Say
Which version of the They Say / I Say routine (with visual texts or with annotation) feels like the best fit for your students right now? Why?:

I plan on you using this strategy with visual text because I work with intervention groups, and most of my students struggle with reading. I would then eventually move into annotating with text that is below grade level, so students are not struggling with reading the text. Read works and new ELA have different levels of articles I could use. I have also been using an old Rigby nonfiction text/book from our book room.

Describe one way you might adapt the They Say / I Say framework to align with the types of texts your students read most often (e.g., articles, visuals, multimodal texts).:

I would adapt this strategy to text that sparks students' interest. As mentioned above, read works, news ELA, and old Rigby readers could be used with this strategy. One of the Rigby readers I used last week was about sports heroes. I picked this text because our syllable-type decodable story was about playing soccer. I could use this strategy when using paired text with decodable stories. Using they say/ I say with a paired text will help students interact deeply with the text. I think using visuals will help my ELLs develop vocabulary and interact with the English language.

What, if anything, do you need help thinking through as you plan for using They Say / I Say in your classroom?:

When annotating, I might prepare ahead of time highlighted areas for students to respond to. I might also create sentence stems to help ELLs with their responses. I also think working in pairs would help struggling students.

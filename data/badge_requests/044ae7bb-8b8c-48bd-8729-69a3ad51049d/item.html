Badge: Reading to Write: They Say / I Say
Which version of the They Say / I Say routine (with visual texts or with annotation) feels like the best fit for your students right now? Why?:

I plan to start using the They Say / I Say framework with visual texts instead of written ones. My students really respond to images, short clips, and graphic organizers, so starting with those helps ease them into deeper conversations. Since we’re about to start our Holocaust unit, beginning with visuals will help them form more meaningful and thoughtful responses. The topic is sensitive and can bring up a lot of emotions, so I think visuals will make the material feel more approachable. Once they’re comfortable, we can work our way toward annotating written texts.

Describe one way you might adapt the They Say / I Say framework to align with the types of texts your students read most often (e.g., articles, visuals, multimodal texts).:

One thing I’m going to try is a quick annotation routine in their notebooks using simple visual organizers. They’ll label one side “They Say” and the other “I Say,” and we’ll practice with historical photos, quotes, and short video clips. I’ll also have them talk with a partner before writing. This gives them a chance to sort out their ideas out loud—something my students do really well—before putting them into the template.

Looking back on this inquiry cycle, what did you learn about your teaching and your students that you might not have noticed otherwise? How did engaging in this teacher-research process shape or shift your thinking about your classroom practice?:

I learned that my students need a lot of modeling prior to letting them try this on their own! I assumed that students would be able to mind map/ make a concept map on their own; however, this is the part my students struggled with the most. It also showed that in my teaching, I need to be direct with my instructions alongside my modeling. Engaging in this teacher- researcher process really challenged the way I present materials to my classroom. I think trying new things can be difficult, but I could see myself implementing more annotation of texts within my biology classroom.

Think back to a moment during the lesson sequence that stands out. It could be a comment from a student, an unexpected challenge or success, a shift in engagement, or a moment when your own thinking changed. Describe what happened and why this moment feels meaningful to you now. What does it reveal:
I overheard in a student's conversation about one of the texts, "Where do they get this DNA anyways?". These type of questions open up new avenues of discussion, and new opportunities for me to teach things that aren't exactly on standard. An unexpected challenge for me is how my students struggled to create a visual representation, such as a concept map. This entire process revealed that my students have the endurance to annotate multiple texts and analyze how those pieces of evidence can apply to their own opinions.

Name: Renee Garner
Badge: Layering Reading, Writing and Thinking
How does the Layering Reading, Writing, and Discussion approach compare to how you have previously supported students in engaging with multiple texts or teaching argument writing? How might this process shift or refine the way you guide students toward forming their own claims?:

Honestly, I have always had students find just one text with evidence to support their argument writing. I feel like I have seen a whole new way to teach kids to approach their argument by giving them multiple texts and having them reflect on multiple perspectives with quick writes and sharing of discussions while reading texts. I don't see myself teaching argumentative writing the same way again! I will be working this summer on building a database of articles for students that present both sides of the argument for all of the topics I present to them.

How would you describe the role of talk and discussion in your classroom right now? What kinds of opportunities do students have to rehearse ideas through conversation? What challenges have you encountered in making talk a meaningful part of the writing process?:
In my classroom we use turn and talk a lot. However, typically our turn and talk ideas are reflective about what has occurred in a story or student inferences they may make while reading, etc. The idea of rehearsing student ideas through conversation is a phenomenal concept that, when I tried with my students, really allowed them to converse appropriately and share/reflect on one another's ideas. The only challenge I've had so far would be, somehow, the two most stubborn and argumentative students in my homeroom ended up paired together. However, I sat back and allowed them to discuss and, with minor reminders and prompting to relax and listen, they did well.

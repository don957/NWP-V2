Name: Shannon Tuley
Badge: Joining a Conversation
What is one insight you gained from completing the Atwoodian Table yourself that might shape how you introduce it to students?:

I cannot wait to do this. I think this is a great way to teach students to look at differing perspectives not as "arguing," but more so as a conversation, which is the way it should be. I can see how this can open their minds to others' thinking in a way that is casual and friendly, and will help them "see both sides."

How does the Atwoodian Table idea compare to how you have taught argument and/or responding to multiple texts in the past?:
This is different than how I have taught argument writing in the past. I used to give them charts and sentence frames to use to write their initial opinion on the topic, and then what they think about the topic after reading each text. It worked, but this simplifies it and the visual aspect will help them much more.
What do you notice about the two sets of excerpts in this module (children in fancy restaurants and toddlers and technology)? How do the excerpts work together to invite students to join the conversation? What are some conversations related to topics you teach?:
I noticed that both sets are relevant to me - I have kids. I noticed that there were texts that cover both sides of the issue and some even included some balance. This invites students to join the conversation because they weren't "black or white" on the opinions of the issue. For example, The toddler and technology texts weren't like "you should never let your kids watch a screen." There were suggested limits and warnings about what could happen if they got too much screen time.

Badge: Reading to Write: They Say / I Say
Which version of the They Say / I Say routine (with visual texts or with annotation) feels like the best fit for your students right now? Why?:

The They Say / I Say routine with visual texts feels like the best fit for my students right now. Visuals immediately grab their attention and lower the barrier to entry for reluctant writers. My students are more willing to share observations and opinions when they can point to something they see, rather than starting with a dense written text. The images give everyone a common starting point, help spark discussion, and make it easier for students to form a “They Say” idea before adding their own “I Say” response. This approach supports engagement while still building the skills needed for later text-based annotation.

Describe one way you might adapt the They Say / I Say framework to align with the types of texts your students read most often (e.g., articles, visuals, multimodal texts).:

One way I might adapt the They Say / I Say framework is by using it with short informational articles paired with visuals (charts, photos, captions), since my students read a lot of multimodal texts. For the They Say part, students could identify the main idea or claim from the article’s headline, subheading, or a key caption. For the I Say part, they would respond by agreeing, disagreeing, or adding on, using evidence from the visual or a specific sentence in the article. This adaptation supports comprehension, helps students practice citing evidence in different formats, and makes the routine feel more accessible and relevant to the types of texts they already encounter.

What, if anything, do you need help thinking through as you plan for using They Say / I Say in your classroom?:

I may need help thinking through how to scaffold the routine for students who struggle with writing and how to gradually move them toward more independent responses. Specifically, I’d like support with creating strong sentence starters, deciding how much modeling is enough, and determining when students are ready to transition from visual-based responses to text-only annotation. I may also need help figuring out how to keep the routine consistent while varying it enough to stay engaging across different types of texts.

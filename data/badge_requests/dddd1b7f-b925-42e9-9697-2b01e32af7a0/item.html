Looking back on this inquiry cycle, what did you learn about your teaching and your students that you might not have noticed otherwise? How did engaging in this teacher-research process shape or shift your thinking about your classroom practice?:

I realized my students are capable of much deeper thinking and stronger connections than I sometimes give them credit for. When I slowed down and incorporated structured annotation more consistently, I noticed students engaging more thoughtfully with the text and supporting their ideas with stronger evidence. The kids also love annotating! They are quickly becoming pros. Annotations are not just as a strategy I love to use, but also a tool that strengthens comprehension and critical thinking. It has helped me become more intentional about creating space for students to process, question, and connect their learning.

What new questions or next steps are you leaving this module with? How might you continue to explore or build on this inquiry in your future teaching?:

After this module I have been thinking a lot about quality instruction and how important it is. I used to rush through activities and foundational skills because I felt they were tedious or should have been mastered a long time ago. Critical thinking is important and by focusing on my quality of instruction, I have been able to provide more authentic work. Reflecting on teacher-research, I would like to explore more ways to continue enhancing reading and writing from multiple angles. I will continue using these strategies because my kids responded so well to all of them!

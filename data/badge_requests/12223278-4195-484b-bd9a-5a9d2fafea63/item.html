Badge: Reading to Write: They Say / I Say
Which version of the They Say / I Say routine (with visual texts or with annotation) feels like the best fit for your students right now? Why?:

The version of the They Say / I Say routine that feels like the best fit for my students right now is using visual texts. Visual texts are easier for my students to understand and engage with, especially because they provide clear, concrete information to work from. Annotation often requires higher-level thinking skills—such as identifying key ideas, summarizing, and interpreting text—which can be challenging for some students at this stage. Starting with visual texts helps build confidence and comprehension, while also laying the groundwork for more advanced annotation skills later on.

Describe one way you might adapt the They Say / I Say framework to align with the types of texts your students read most often (e.g., articles, visuals, multimodal texts).:

One way I might adapt the They Say / I Say framework in my agriculture classroom is by using short historical agriculture texts or visuals about major agricultural events, such as the invention of the cotton gin or the seed drill. After identifying what the text says, students respond with I say by analyzing the event from a specific perspective—such as a consumer, farmer, or scientist. This approach helps students engage more deeply with the material, connects content to real-world impacts, and allows them to practice interpreting information through different roles while still working with the types of texts they encounter most often in agriculture.

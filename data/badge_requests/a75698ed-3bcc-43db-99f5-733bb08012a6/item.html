Name: shelly strickfaden
Badge: Layering Reading, Writing and Thinking
How does the Layering Reading, Writing, and Discussion approach compare to how you have previously supported students in engaging with multiple texts or teaching argument writing? How might this process shift or refine the way you guide students toward forming their own claims?:

This is a different approach to argument writing. In the past, I have started with a claim and helped students find evidence to support or disprove the claim. This seems to start with information that can be used to develop a claim and the claim comes last. It seems opposite from the way I have taught it, but that could be better as it may let student form real opinions rather than trying to put down the right idea. I like that this process starts small and then builds and incorporates different opinions. I think it will help guide students to developing their own opinions because they will be presented with a range of information and then have to figure out what is important to them.

How would you describe the role of talk and discussion in your classroom right now? What kinds of opportunities do students have to rehearse ideas through conversation? What challenges have you encountered in making talk a meaningful part of the writing process?:
My students mostly talk to answer questions or share thoughts. They haven't really talked to develop their opinions in this way before. Often I find my students are less willing to share their opinions. They like to chat with others, but they don't want to be wrong. I think this method could be good as students will be working through the info together.

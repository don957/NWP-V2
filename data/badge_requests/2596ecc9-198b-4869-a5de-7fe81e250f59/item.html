What is one insight you gained from completing the Atwoodian Table yourself that might shape how you introduce it to students?:

The physical representation of the table and chairs is great, but I liked the idea shared by another teacher that brings it to life in the classroom! This idea of organizing what has been said, others that have added, and then adding their own thinking would really enhance a student's experience with debatable topics! I also thought using large butcher paper or chart paper would make this a doable Placemat Conversation in which students could work in small groups and collaborate to add the thinking to the paper. This would let them try it together before being expected to do this independently.

How does the Atwoodian Table idea compare to how you have taught argument and/or responding to multiple texts in the past?:
This is similar to the PAPA square, however, this brings in more than one text/author/perspective to the conversation rather than focusing on just one. I really like this as I believe students could see the different source info clearly.
What do you notice about the two sets of excerpts in this module (children in fancy restaurants and toddlers and technology)? How do the excerpts work together to invite students to join the conversation? What are some conversations related to topics you teach?:
They both involved small children, and, surprisingly, MS & HS students have some passionate opinions on! Additionally, adolescents would have experienced some of these situations and would be able to connect with the conversations more directly.

I'm struggling with this strategy. I get it for reading things like To Kill a Mockingbird, a poem, or something else in literature where the meaning isn't always clear. In science, what they say, they mean, at least in my way of thinking. I thought I would use this strategy for my Module 4, then changed my mind when I couldn't figure out personally what I would write for Say and Mean. Maybe I would combine the two columns (Say/Mean) and (Matter). This would help students focus on both what they are saying, and what it means...then figure out why it matters.

To answer the original question, I would like to introduce this to students using non-fiction text from Science World magazine. We used the They Say I Say strategy, which worked very well, but I can advance this by having them partner read (we read the long article out loud in class), and figure out ahead of time how many bullet points they would need. Seventh graders need that kind of scaffolding. The partner reading would be another step on the ladder toward them reading a long non-fiction article on their own.

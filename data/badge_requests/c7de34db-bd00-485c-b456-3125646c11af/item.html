Looking back on this inquiry cycle, what did you learn about your teaching and your students that you might not have noticed otherwise? How did engaging in this teacher-research process shape or shift your thinking about your classroom practice?:

I think I've learned or been reminded that breaking big assignments into manageable chunks really results in quality work. Doing this, as well as providing an ongoing support (slide show continuously pulled up as they worked), seemed to foster their creativity and passion. They were comfortable, and did not feel overwhelmed. In addition, they were passionate; this was a topic they already had opinions about, and they were encouraged to express those opinions. I did remind them several times that they had absorbed the sources, so they needed to think of themselves as the experts. This felt empowering, and they responded very well. I was reminded to be sure to provided those visual reminders throughout their lessons and activities, so they can refer to them as they need or want. I also look forward to shifting my research and argumentative approaches a bit to accomodate instructions of this nature. Digestible pieces that are empowering are something to look forward to!

Think back to a moment during the lesson sequence that stands out. It could be a comment from a student, an unexpected challenge or success, a shift in engagement, or a moment when your own thinking changed. Describe what happened and why this moment feels meaningful to you now. What does it reveal:
It might seem odd, but the moment when I realized that my students were really struggling to understand the task of creating a visual was a moment of reflection. For many years, I've noticed that students have increasing struggles to think outside the box and create certain pieces from scratch. This reminded me of that. I want to begin to push them to think outside the box from day one next year. That's a big deal to me, because I really believe it will affect their performance in many areas. I also hope they'll gain in confidence. It revealed to me something I need to pay attention to and address as a teacher, so I'm already thinking about ways to be better.
As you look over the drafts your students produced in this lesson sequence, what early patterns, surprises, or questions are emerging for you? What aspects of your students’ writing are you most curious to explore more deeply with colleagues during our April regroup? What, if any, questions do you h:
Although it really isn't the "big picture" of this sequence, I'm seeing patterns of continued lack of grammar and general writing skills. I do teach lessons on paragraphing, grammar, pov, and many other elements of writing throughout the year, but students seem to forget all of those lessons when it's time to actually produce a written piece. I wonder how students will respond to a Layered Reading lesson sequence based on the novel we're currently reading, and I look forward to finding out. I do look forward to discussing with colleagues the struggles students have with producing visuals, and hope to hear some insights to inspire my own students. Thank goodness, my students were very patient and appreciated the fact that we were all doing this for the first time. I think being open and honest with about that helped us move forward as best we all could. It was an excellent experience!

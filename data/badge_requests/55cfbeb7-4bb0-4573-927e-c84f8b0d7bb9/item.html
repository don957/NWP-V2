Looking back on this inquiry cycle, what did you learn about your teaching and your students that you might not have noticed otherwise? How did engaging in this teacher-research process shape or shift your thinking about your classroom practice?:

I really loved this layered writing activity. In the past, I have really discredited the value of giving kids time to summarize and free write about lessons. It was always about getting to the next standard or concept, but this strategy taught me that kids are more engaged when we can give them this summary and reflection time, and I can better assess where my students are as individuals.

Think back to a moment during the lesson sequence that stands out. It could be a comment from a student, an unexpected challenge or success, a shift in engagement, or a moment when your own thinking changed. Describe what happened and why this moment feels meaningful to you now. What does it reveal:
The most meaningful moments to me during this lesson sequence was overhearing students talk to their partners. Since our texts revolved around political discourse and the inability of people to communicate who vary on the political spectrum, it was interesting to see how many of my students agreed with this and felt genuinely tapped out of political atmospheres because of how polarizing they have seen them be while growing up. Many even reflected on how much better our country would be if people could just talk to each other and see each other as human rather than a political side. For me, this reveals how many of my students want a better functioning and more unified country, and that give me hope they will work to make that happen.

When I was reviewing my students drafts, I realized that a few of my students still really struggle with the synthesis concept, especially when we tied the text to Julius Caesar. I do think this could potentially be some poor explaining on my part perhaps, but I am eager to get some feedback in this space during the Write the Learn Conference meet-up. I feel I am sometimes inclined to be too helpful with my students and that, in turn, makes them entirely way too dependent on me. I am really motivated by Jennifer Fletcher’s Writing Rhetorically, and I want to back away from having my students so dependent on me. Therefore, I am not sure how much of their struggle was normal struggle that comes from developing independence as a writer and how much was my errors in effectively teaching synthesis-style writing.

Name: Renee Garner
Badge: Reading to Write: They Say / I Say
Which version of the They Say / I Say routine (with visual texts or with annotation) feels like the best fit for your students right now? Why?:

I think both versions would be helpful for my students. I can imagine that using visual texts to introduce the routine would be easier as my students, as far as I know, have never really annotated or been asked to think in this way. I would definitely need to use a couple of visual texts or shorter articles with a lot of guidance for them to feel comfortable and successful.

Describe one way you might adapt the They Say / I Say framework to align with the types of texts your students read most often (e.g., articles, visuals, multimodal texts).:

I envision using this routine heavily when we are working on argumentative essay writing. During this time, I present a variety of articles for students that have differing opinions and facts from two sides of a topic. I can envision the They Say/I Say routine helping students express and organize their own viewpoints on varying topics.

What, if anything, do you need help thinking through as you plan for using They Say / I Say in your classroom?:

I think planning for timing and finding factual resources that might be more in-depth and contain varying perspectives from what I already have might be something I need to think through. Student prior experience, or lack of, with annotation is also something that I need to think about.

Name: Angie Fulton
Badge: Reading to Write: They Say / I Say
Which version of the They Say / I Say routine (with visual texts or with annotation) feels like the best fit for your students right now? Why?:

I think both routines are beneficial for my students. It is important in science to accurately be able to understand what is depicted in visual data. The visual text will help students slow down and really "see" what information is being represented. I hope with practice, they will be able to find bias and flaws in the data, as well as reflect on what trends and connections the data shows.

Annotation is also important to science, so this lower stakes but intentional conversation with the texts will help some see the importance of annotation, or even help them understand what annotation looks like. Students often don't annotate because they feel like it is overwhelming or they don't want to do it wrong. This allows them to express their thoughts on the text without high stakes.

Describe one way you might adapt the They Say / I Say framework to align with the types of texts your students read most often (e.g., articles, visuals, multimodal texts).:

I will probably use this most with articles, however I hope to include more multimodal and visual texts in all of the subjects I teach. Giving them a visual and having them analyze it by finding patterns would be a good way to start a chemistry or biology concept. I could see this being fun if we do this for safety in the laboratory images. They can interact with all the right and wrong things they see depicted.

What, if anything, do you need help thinking through as you plan for using They Say / I Say in your classroom?:

I think I need to plan and think through what material I want to use this for and which units it will work best for. Exemplars in science would also be helpful.

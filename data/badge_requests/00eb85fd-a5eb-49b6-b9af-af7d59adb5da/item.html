Name: Suzann Copeland
Badge: Reading to Write: They Say / I Say
Which version of the They Say / I Say routine (with visual texts or with annotation) feels like the best fit for your students right now? Why?:

With my struggling students I really think we need to start at the spot with the visuals, media, etc. They are really engaged with visuals and get started working faster. An English class I go in and co-teach with is using annotation with texts already, and using colors to annotate feelings, things that you have in common, and things that are different in your life.

Describe one way you might adapt the They Say / I Say framework to align with the types of texts your students read most often (e.g., articles, visuals, multimodal texts).:

We are doing this in the English classes I co-teach in. We adapt to the types of responses we use. With my struggling kiddos we need to start with visuals and multi-modal texts. We might start with how does this make you feel?

What, if anything, do you need help thinking through as you plan for using They Say / I Say in your classroom?:

My co-teacher and I are super pumped about using this--- it is easy to use with consumable texts--- but what about on the novels you use yearly? We've thought through this a little bit and are using post it notes to annotate on. But we are going to try to let the folks just annotate away in pencil on our crucible books for the next year's students to think about and to respond to.

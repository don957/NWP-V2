SMLC Overview

This National Writing Project yearlong course is designed to support teachers in the Missouri Show Me Literacies Collaborative as they plan to integrate source-based argument writing in their specific teaching context. Over the course of the 2023-24 school year, participants in this course will examine their own teaching practices, experience high-leverage instructional approaches, and engage with practical and strategic applications for the classroom—all focused on the specific teaching of source-based argument writing.

As is the case with all courses in the Teaching Argument Writing community, this course was developed based on the research and design of NWP’s College, Career, and Community Writer’s Program (C3WP), a program that has been proven to have a statistically significant impact on students’ argument writing in three randomized-control trials.

During this first course in the elementary strand, we will explore a variety of ways to help you integrate Routine Argument Writing into your teaching context.

☑️ Course Certificate

This certificate is for 12 learning hours, to be completed by September 24th. To earn the certificate for this course participants will need to earn the following micro-credentials:

  • Find arguments in the world and in your own life

  • Interact with texts using They Say / I Say and Say–Mean–Matter

  • Create your own Atwoodian Table

  • Practice writing and revising a claim

  • Post to all discussions, respond to others, and submit a reflection about your experience in the discussions

  • Write a final reflection about your course experience

💬 Introduce Yourself

Throughout this course, you’ll be invited to participate in the discussions and respond to the posts of others. If you have not already done so, please introduce yourself in the discussion and connect with the course community.

The community discussions will be a valuable resource and a way to share your work in progress and get feedback from your colleagues; it's also a great place to gather ideas and resources from others.

❓Why Routine Argument Writing?

Before we get started, take a few minutes to check out this video that explains the value of using Routine Argument Writing in our classrooms.

Now that we have explored the why, let’s get started with the how. One way to integrate Routine Argument Writing in your classroom is to help students recognize that they are already surrounded by arguments! With a little bit of writing time, maybe in their writing notebooks or as part of daily quickwrites, students can practice gathering topics, responding to images, and writing claims. After practicing regularly, students will begin to see themselves as argument makers. Read on and try out some easy entry activities for exploring argument topics in our own lives.

Course Modules

As is the case with all courses and lesson studies in the yearlong course series, this course is organized by "modules." Each module is designed to involve approximately three hours of self-paced coursework. The course is organized in this way to support your planning for completing the course between now and September 24th. You have access to the entire course, so feel free to work at a pace that works best for you and your schedule! The first SMLC lesson study is scheduled to begin on October 9th.

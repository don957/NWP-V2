Eradicating Mosquitos

DAY 1

Welcome to the Eradicating Mosquitoes social studies and science text set for the Making Claims resource! Try this resource out with your students to support them as they practice writing recursive claims. The lesson sequence layers annotated reading, reflective writing, and critical thinking to guide students as they gather information from texts, consider multiple angles on a topic, develop and revise a claim, and draft an 11-minute argument.

Full Text Set

The texts listed below will be introduced within the daily lessons. They are provided here to share with students, but you may want to wait and introduce them one at a time as you proceed through the lesson sequence.

Text #1: Infographic describing how genetically modified mosquitoes are used to reduce mosquito populations. “Figure 5. How genetically modified mosquito (GM) releases reduce mosquito numbers and mosquito-transmitted disease incidence.” (University of Florida, 2021).

Text #2: Science magazine news article describing the introduction of genetically modified mosquitoes in the U.S. The article describes some of the pushback from community members. “First genetically modified mosquitoes released in the United States.” (Nature, 2021).

Text #3: Informational web article for young people about diseases spread through contact with mosquitoes. “Mosquito-borne diseases.” (Kids Health, 2019).

Text #4: News article about scientific efforts to decrease mosquito populations. “Fighting mosquitoes with mosquitoes: Biological weapons target Zika virus” (Los Angeles Times, 2016).

Text #5: Reader-submitted opinions in a science magazine on possible consequences of killing off all mosquitoes and whether it’s possible. “If mosquitoes were eradicated, what would be the consequences?” (New Scientist, 2021)


​Day 1 Lesson Sequence

QUICKWRITE

First, lead students through the quickwrite activity slides below. This activity gets students thinking and talking about the topic. They write to acknowledge that we come to an idea, topic, or issue with a gut claim, feeling, or belief. It’s important for teachers to not share their own opinions on the topic so students may develop their own ability to make informed claims.


READ TEXT #1

Next, observe the infographic “Figure 5. How genetically modified mosquito (GM) releases reduce mosquito numbers and mosquito-transmitted disease incidence.” from the University of Florida (Text #1) with students, discussing what the text says about Eradicating Mosquitoes. Students may use the “Reading” (left) side of their notebook to capture what they see (images, words, colors, placement, information, etc.).

READ TEXT #2

With students, read, interpret, and annotate the text “First genetically modified mosquitoes released in the United States.” by Nature (Text #2). Students may use the “Reading” (left) side of their notebook to capture what they see (images, words, colors, placement, information, etc.).

RESPOND

Students should now write on the “Writing” (right) side of their notebook. What are they thinking about the Eradicating Mosquitoes after reading Text #1 and #2? You may share your thinking with students as a model.

DISCUSS

Lead the students in a discussion about the topic. What are they thinking? Consider strategies here for discussion, such as a digital whiteboard discussion or an oral small/large group discussion. Note again, the teacher should avoid sharing their opinions here.

ADD TO WRITING

Ask students to use these sentence frames or others they might think of to add to their writing:

The article/graph/quote makes me question...

Now I am thinking...

I’m wondering...

Just as I was thinking earlier...

Students add to their initial writing about the topic with new information from the text. Guide students to include specific details from the text as they add to their writing.

PROCEED TO DAY 2

DAY 2

The second day of the lesson introduces students to two additional texts with opportunities to read, annotate, and write.

Day 2 Lesson Sequence

WRITING INTO THE DAY

Ask students to re-read their final written entry from the day before. Ask them to add any thoughts or questions to their thinking from yesterday.

READ & ANNOTATE TEXT #3

Provide students with Text #3, “Mosquito-borne diseases.” from Kids Health. Ask students to use the following annotation strategies while reading the text.

  • Students should place a ✭ (star) next to key insights they notice in the article.

  • Students should use an “F” for each fact they notice that seems important or compelling.

WRITE/ANNOTATE

Next, ask students to add 2-3 reasons from the article that supports their thinking about Eradicating Mosquitoes. Their responses should be added on the “Reading” (left) side of their notebook.

READ & ANNOTATE TEXT #4

Provide students with Text #4, “Fighting mosquitoes with mosquitoes: Biological weapons target Zika virus” from the Los Angeles Times. Ask students to use the following annotation strategies while reading the text.

  • Students should place a ✭ (star) next to key insights they notice in the article.

  • Students should use an “F” for each fact they notice that seems important or compelling.

WRITE/ANNOTATE

Next, ask students to choose two or three of the coded annotations they made while reading the texts above. On the “Reading” (left) side of the notebook, ask students to write a brief explanation about why they annotated what they did.

ADD TO WRITING

Ask students to add to their writing about the topic with new information from today’s readings. Guide students to include references to specific insights and facts from the articles as they add to their writing. This writing is the first step in formulating an informed claim about the issue.

PROCEED TO DAY 3

DAY 3

In the third day of the lesson, students read and annotate Text #5 and add to their writing on the topic.

Day 3 Lesson Sequence

WRITING INTO THE DAY

Ask students to review their writer’s notebook from Day 2. Next, invite student to pair-share what they discovered from reading the first three texts, using their notebooks to guide their sharing. Ask students to write a summary of what their partner shared.

READ & ANNOTATE TEXT #5

Provide students with Text #5, “If mosquitoes were eradicated, what would be the consequences?” from the New Scientist. Ask students to use the following annotation strategies while reading the text.

  • Students should place a ✭ (star) next to key insights they notice in the article.

  • Students should use an “F” for each fact they notice that seems important or compelling.

WRITE/ANNOTATE

Next, ask students to choose 2-3 annotations from Text #5 to write about in their notebooks. Their responses should be added on the "Reading" (left) side. Invite students to use the sentence frames below to explain their choices.

The quote makes me question...

Now I am thinking...

I’m wondering...

Just as I was thinking earlier...

Students add to their initial writing about the topic with new information from the text. Guide students to include specific details from the text as they add to their writing.

PROCEED TO DAY 4

DAY 4

Day 4 Lesson Sequence

WRITE

Ask students to create an It Says/I Say T-chart in their notebook, like this:

As you facilitate a whole-class discussion on what students have learned about Eradicating Mosquitoes, you can draw this out on the board and demonstrate how to fill in each side of the chart. It is important that students see how you choose texts for the "it Says" column, and hear your thinking in response to that text in the "I Say" column. Ask students to copy 2-3 of their underlined or coded passages from Texts 1-4 on the left (“It Says”) side of the chart. For each passage added on the left, students should respond to that passage on the right (“I Say”) side of the chart.

​WRITING & REVISING CLAIMS

INITIAL CLAIM

Ask students to look through the chart and writing they have created on the topic and write an initial claim about Eradicating Mosquitoes in their notebook, informed by their reading. Encourage them to think beyond pro/con or yes/no. Provide students a list of hedging or qualifying words on the board that are often used in academic writing to strengthen claims, such as: probably, usually, should, most likely, and often. Have students write their claim by stating their complex position on Eradicating Mosquitoes. They should label this claim in the notebook as their “initial claim.” The slideshow above can be used to help teach students what a claim is and how to write their initial claim.

SHARE

Ask for several students to read their claims and share one piece of evidence from the texts that helped them to make that claim.

PROCEED TO DAY 5

DAY 5

Day 5 Lesson Sequence

REVISING THE CLAIM

Invite students to review their initial claim and remind them they can revise the claims they have written. Discuss why one might choose to revise their initial claim.

WRITING AN ARGUMENT

Using the process below, guide students through an 11-minute argument draft (adapted from Gretchen Bernabei). Students will write this on the “Writing” side of their notebook. Consider modeling your own draft on a digital whiteboard or chart paper as students write in their notebooks.

  • For 1 minute: Write down your gut reaction about Eradicating Mosquitoes. This is how you felt when we first started talking about the topic.

  • For 4 minutes: Write about what things you should consider about Eradicating Mosquitoes. Think about what we have learned and what you already wrote about in your writer’s notebook.

  • For 4 minutes: Write about your own position. What is your claim about Eradicating Mosquitoes.

  • For 2 minutes: Write, “Now I am thinking...” and share what you are now thinking about Eradicating Mosquitoes. Or, write, “Maybe we should...” and share what you think we should do about Eradicating Mosquitoes.

  • Share! Read your argument to a partner.

OPTIONAL STRUCTURES

You may want to provide other possible structures for the organization that make sense when writing an argument. Use options from the handout below, adapted from Gretchen Bernabei’s Kernel Essays. ADDITIONAL TEXTS

Use the additional texts below as needed to layer information and perspectives about the topic of Eradicating Mosquitoes.

Science research brief (with infographics) about genetically modified mosquitoes. Posted to a university website for a public audience. “Genetically modified mosquitoes.” (University of Florida, 2021).

Podcast episode about the potential impacts of eradicating mosquitoes. “Kill ‘em all.” (RadioLab, 2014).

University news article about how climate change will allow mosquito populations (and the diseases they carry) to move further north in the United States. “Climate change will expose more people to risk of mosquito-borne disease.” (University of Florida, 2019).

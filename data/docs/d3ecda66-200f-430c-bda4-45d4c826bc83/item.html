Trying out the Quick List and Writing Sprints strategy with my students taught me how powerful low-stakes writing can be for students. I learned that when students don't have time to overthink they will write more freely and express more ideas. The quick lists gave them a starting point to start their writing sprints. This pushed them past the stage of "I don't know what to write." Many of them were surprised by how quickly ideas surfaced once they started making a list. They said this strategy made writing feel less intimidating. What surprised me was that even after the writing sprints students wanted to keep writing and adding more details to their writing. Next, I'd like to build on this strategy by using this strategy as a pre-writing routine more consistently. I want to try having students create quick lists with longer pieces of writing, such as a narrative.

The most important detail that I’ve learned from this trial is the importance of previewing an activity. The approach that I bring to the content will affect the work and output my students return.

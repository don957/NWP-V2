Course OVERVIEW: Breakdown byModule

As is the case with all courses and lesson studies in the yearlong course series, this course is organized by "chapters." Each chapter is designed to involve approximately three hours of self-paced coursework. The course is organized in this way to support your planning for completing the course between now and September 22nd. You have access to the entire course, so feel free to work at a pace that works best for you and your schedule! The first SMLC lesson study is scheduled to begin on October 7th.

  • Chapter One: Exploring Argument in our Lives

  • Chapter Two: Arguments in Response to Single Texts

  • Chapter Three: Arguments in Response to Multiple Texts

  • Chapter Four: Skill Spotlight: Writing and revising claims

❓Why Routine Argument Writing?

Before we get started, take a few minutes to check out this video that explains the value of using Routine Argument Writing in our classrooms.

Now that we have explored the why, let’s get started with the how. One way to integrate Routine Argument Writing in your classroom is to help students recognize that they are already surrounded by arguments! With a little bit of writing time, maybe in their writing notebooks or as part of daily quickwrites, students can practice gathering topics, responding to images, and writing claims. After practicing regularly, students will begin to see themselves as argument makers. Read on and try out some easy entry activities for exploring argument topics in our own lives.

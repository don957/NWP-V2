SMLC Source-Based Argument

Course Overview

Welcome to the 2024-25 Secondary Source-Based Argument course! This National Writing Project (NWP) yearlong course is designed to support teachers in the Missouri Show Me Literacies Collaborative (SMLC) as they plan for integrating source-based argument writing in their specific teaching context.

NWP's Source-Based Argument course is an online professional learning opportunity designed to equip teachers in Missouri with effective strategies and resources for teaching source-based argument. Over the course of the 2024-25 school year, participants in this course will examine their own teaching practices, experience high-leverage instructional approaches, and engage with practical and strategic applications for the classroom—all focused on the specific teaching of source-based argument writing.

As is the case with all courses in the Teaching Argument Writing community, this course was developed based on the research and design of NWP’s College, Career, and Community Writer’s Program (C3WP), a program that has been proven to have a statistically significant impact on students’ argument writing in three randomized-control trials.

Course Objectives

Participants will engage in interactive discussions, try out classroom learning experiences, design engaging activities, and receive feedback and support from a dedicated National Writing Project thinking partner. By the end of the course, teachers will have gained valuable insights into teaching source-based argument and will be able to apply these strategies in their own classrooms.

Throughout the course, participants will:

  1. Explore NWP's approach to teaching source-based argument.

  2. Try out a range of source-based argument learning experiences and reflect on the implications for their own teaching.

  3. Adapt and create instructional activities that promote student engagement and collaboration.

  4. Plan for, teach, and reflect on their teaching of source based argument lesson sequences.

  5. Implement effective formative assessment practices for analyzing student writing to inform next steps in instruction.

  6. Reflect on personal teaching practices and adapt instructional strategies accordingly.

  7. Collaborate with colleagues to share best practices and resources for teaching source-based argument.

Course Structure

The course series includes a range of learning experiences, including real-time Zoom events, self-paced coursework, and collaborative lesson studies. The courses are designed to provide opportunities to collaborate with other teachers who are participating in the program, but with flexibility for you to make it work in your specific teaching context.

Additional Information

As you probably know, this online course is one part of the larger Show Me Literacies Collaborative (SMLC). Below is additional information about some key components of this program outside of the online courses. This section was built based on frequently asked questions from teachers who participated in the online courses in the last two school years.

The Missouri Writing Project Network: This network is made up of three Writing Projects in Missouri: Gateway Writing Project at UMSL, Greater KC Writing Project at UCM, and Missouri Writing Project at MU. Depending on your region, you will be supported by one of these three Writing Project sites. Among other things, this support includes local Summer Institutes, local Regroup Meetings, and face-to-face coaching.

Fall/Spring Prompt Administration: In the fall and spring you will be invited to engage your students in a 2-day writing prompt to gather student writing as part of the research into this program. All writings will be gathered anonymously and will only be used to help assess the effectiveness of the NWP SMLC Program. Note that the fall (pre-test) prompts are baseline prompts which are meant to be administered as early in the year as possible, ideally before you have taught any argument writing for the year. Prompt Administration windows are as follows.

  • Fall 2024: August 19 - Sept 28, 2024

  • Spring 2025: March 9 - May 13, 2025

Using Sources Tool (UST): NWP's Using Sources Tool (UST) is a formative assessment tool that teachers use to provide a focused look at the quality of students’ claims and how they select and use evidence from sources. The tool combines a series of scaled questions related to use of source material and a short narrative question to name next instructional steps. Using this tool has resulted in lively and productive conversations among teachers, supporting them in identifying next teaching steps for their students. At your local Regroup meetings you will receive training in the UST, which involve collaboratively analyzing your student writing from the fall and spring lesson studies with the UST.
